## Goal

Make the following the **top-level documented architecture principle of Qleisli**:

> **Policy as Theorem. Constitution as Harness. Compiler as Executor.**

These three phrases name three distinct layers.

### Policy as Theorem

A Qleisli policy that affects constitutional acceptance should not remain an implementation convention, prose expectation, or maintainer preference.

It should ultimately become a mechanically checkable proof obligation.

The Constitution does not itself freeze the mathematical content of QS, PR, or RS. Under #129 Draft 5, the human Constitution Guardian Office determines the binding constitutional interpretation for a defined scope; that interpretation is then formalized as the proposition or evidence that must be discharged.

Thus:

> **Policy becomes authoritative through human constitutional interpretation, and becomes enforceable mathematics through theorem-level formalization.**

### Constitution as Harness

The Constitution permanently fixes the existence of the Fundamental-Theorem obligations, the authority and procedure by which their binding meanings are determined, and the non-weakening / guarantee-ledger rules that preserve discharged semantic guarantees across the edition.

The Constitution is therefore not merely descriptive prose around the language.

It is the harness that prevents future syntax, implementations, backends, tools, releases, or formalizations from escaping the obligations or silently weakening accumulated guarantees.

### Compiler as Executor

The compiler is not an independent source of constitutional policy.

It executes the currently authorized language rules, acceptance boundary, and mechanically checkable obligations.

It may reject programs, elaborate source, produce evidence, invoke verification, and teach the boundary through diagnostics, but it does not acquire authority to decide what QS, PR, RS, or the Constitution mean.

The governing rule is:

> **The compiler executes policy; it does not create policy.**

When source crosses a protected classical/quantum, ownership, effect, semantic-authority, resource, or other accepted boundary, compilation must fail closed and explain the violated boundary rather than silently weakening the policy.

The language and ecosystem are the executable surface through which this architecture becomes usable by humans and coding agents.

## Core architectural picture

```text
Qleisli Constitution
        ↓
permanent obligations + non-weakening harness
        ↓
human Constitution Guardian Office
        ↓
binding constitutional interpretation
        ↓
Policy as Theorem
formal proposition / evidence obligation
        ↓
Lean / designated independent proof checker
        ↓
discharged guarantee + acceptance rules
        ↓
Language Reference + Core
        ↓
Compiler as Executor
        ↓
diagnostics, stdlib, qlirgo, QLT/QDB/QCP, tooling
        ↓
human / LLM interaction with one consistent system
```

The authority directions are deliberate:

- the Constitution constrains constitutional interpretation;
- human constitutional authority determines what must be proved;
- formal verification determines whether it has been proved;
- the compiler executes the resulting accepted rules;
- diagnostics and ecosystem tooling expose those rules to users and agents.

No lower layer may silently manufacture authority belonging to a higher layer.

The surface language and ecosystem are therefore not independent product layers. They are **interfaces through which the constitutional structure becomes executable and discoverable**.

## Constitution as a harness

In conventional agent workflows, the harness is often external:

```text
prompt
tests
lint
CI
policy checks
```

Qleisli should instead internalize the critical constraints into the language and acceptance architecture:

```text
constitutional rule
        ↓
language / Core boundary
        ↓
machine-checkable acceptance condition
        ↓
compiler/verifier rejection when violated
        ↓
diagnostic exposing the violated boundary
```

Thus an agent should not need an external prompt to remember fundamental Qleisli rules.

Where a rule is constitutive of Qleisli, the preferred design is:

> **make violating it unrepresentable, untypable, unverifiable, or explicitly rejected.**

External guidance may help usability, but it must not be the only mechanism preserving a constitutional guarantee.

## Compiler as Executor

The compiler should be documented as executing and enforcing the already-authorized boundaries that make Qleisli Qleisli.

It is a boundary enforcer **because it is an executor of policy**, not because it possesses independent semantic or constitutional authority.

Examples include:

- classical vs quantum control;
- affine classical values vs linear quantum ownership;
- `q***` / `Q<T>` / unmarked syntax boundaries;
- legal borrowing / temporary access to quantum owners;
- clean workspace and uncomputation obligations;
- effect boundaries such as Unitary / Iso / Observe;
- primitive vs derived authority;
- ordinary `.qli` vs external artifacts;
- exact phase / axis-order obligations where semantically relevant;
- qrate/module/toolchain boundaries;
- unsupported or unproved semantic claims.

The desired user/agent experience is not:

> “the documentation says this is discouraged.”

It is:

> **“cross this boundary and the compiler/verifier stops you, with a diagnostic that explains why.”**

This is not punishment for its own sake. Rejection is the mechanism by which the constitutional model remains visible and learnable.

## Diagnostics are constitutional pedagogy

Compiler and parser diagnostics are part of the same architecture.

A rejected program should, where possible, expose:

1. which boundary was crossed;
2. what classical or generic prior likely caused the mistake;
3. what Qleisli construct or mental model applies instead;
4. how to recover without weakening the guarantee.

This links directly to #224, #225, #237 and #253.

A predictable mistake should become a route into the Qleisli model, not a dead end.

## Stdlib and ecosystem consequences

This principle explains why ordinary stdlib definitions should be checked `.qli` source (#167).

If a user or LLM does not understand a derived abstraction, it can inspect the source and see the same constitutional constraints applied recursively.

Likewise, first-party tools should either:

- expose the same inferable boundary structure; or
- be explicitly discoverable as Qleisli-native concepts.

No tool may silently create an alternative semantic authority.

## Policy as Theorem and the three Fundamental Theorems

The three Fundamental Theorems are not a proof that one particular Rust compiler implementation is correct, and this architecture document must not independently define their mathematical meaning.

Under #129 Draft 5:

- **Qleisli Soundness Theorem (QS)**,
- **Physical Realizability Theorem (PR)**, and
- **Resource Safety Theorem (RS)**

are permanent and constitutionally distinct proof obligations.

Their binding mathematical interpretation for a scope belongs exclusively to the human Constitution Guardian Office. Their mathematical discharge belongs to Lean or the designated independent proof checker.

Accordingly, this issue, the Language Reference, compiler source, theorem names, tests, and other subordinate artifacts may describe or implement a current binding interpretation, but may not independently create one.

The architectural invariant is instead schematic:

```text
human binding interpretation
        ↓
current constitutional proof obligation Φ
        ↓
Lean / independent checker
        ↓
formal discharge
        ↓
protected guarantee ledger
        ↓
current production acceptance / execution
```

For an accepted scope, the constitutional obligations can be viewed schematically as:

```text
Accepted_E(P) -> QS_E(P) ∧ PR_E(P) ∧ RS_E(P)
```

but this notation is explanatory only. The Constitution and this issue do not freeze the predicates `QS_E`, `PR_E`, or `RS_E`.

This is the intended meaning of **Policy as Theorem**:

> a binding policy is not merely implemented; it is translated into a proposition or evidence obligation whose discharge can be independently checked.

The frontend/compiler implementation may change. The binding interpretations, discharged guarantees, and constitutional non-weakening rules remain the source of semantic continuity.

## Design consequence: reject first, explain second, never weaken silently

When a requested construct cannot be admitted without violating or bypassing a constitutional guarantee, Qleisli should prefer:

1. explicit rejection;
2. precise explanation;
3. a safe expressible alternative where one exists;

over:

- implicit coercion;
- hidden discard/reset;
- silently weaker semantics;
- unchecked backend escape hatches;
- “best effort” acceptance;
- implementation folklore.

A false negative is often repairable. A false acceptance at a constitutional boundary is architectural corruption.

## Documentation requirement

The 0.3.0 docs should contain a short top-level architecture statement, linked prominently from the Language Reference and contributor documentation, that states:

> **Policy as Theorem. Constitution as Harness. Compiler as Executor.**

and explains that:

- binding constitutional policy is turned into formal proof/evidence obligations;
- the Constitution preserves the obligations and accumulated guarantees across the edition;
- the human Constitution Guardian Office determines binding constitutional interpretation;
- Lean / the designated independent checker determines mathematical discharge;
- the compiler executes the authorized language and acceptance rules but does not create constitutional policy;
- compiler/verifier rejection is the ordinary mechanism for boundary enforcement;
- diagnostics teach the violated boundary;
- stdlib and tools must not create parallel semantic authority;
- the three Fundamental Theorems justify the guarantees of the accepted side of the boundary under their binding interpretations;
- compiler implementation correctness is not itself the constitutional theorem claim;
- all future language and ecosystem design should be evaluated against this architecture.

## Design principle

> **Policy as Theorem. Constitution as Harness. Compiler as Executor.**

Expanded:

> **Human constitutional authority determines what must be proved. Policy is formalized as theorem-level obligation. The Constitution prevents semantic escape and backward weakening. Lean checks mathematical discharge. The compiler executes the resulting accepted rules.**
