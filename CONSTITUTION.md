# Qleisli 2026 Constitution

## Preamble

Qleisli may grow through new language features, implementations, libraries, semantic models, and target realizations. Every such development remains subject to the constitutional proof obligations of its edition.

This Constitution establishes enduring proof obligations, continuity rules, and the institutions that interpret and enforce them.

It deliberately does **not** prescribe one permanent mathematical definition, theorem statement, formal representation, quantifier structure, proof method, or implementation of those obligations.

The Constitution fixes what Qleisli must never cease to answer for. The mathematics by which those obligations are made precise belongs to constitutional interpretation and formal verification.

## Article I — The Fundamental Theorems

Qleisli edition 2026 recognizes three permanent Fundamental-Theorem proof obligations from the time of ratification:

1. **Qleisli Soundness Theorem (QS)**
2. **Physical Realizability Theorem (PR)**
3. **Resource Safety Theorem (RS)**

These three obligations are permanent and constitutionally distinct.

The Constitution does not itself define their mathematical content. In particular, it does not fix their predicates, quantifiers, semantic domains, target conditions, resource measures, theorem types, proof strategies, completeness properties, or exclusions.

Their binding mathematical interpretation is determined under Articles V and VI by the **Constitution Guardian Office**.

Their mathematical discharge is established by Lean or the designated independent proof checker under the recorded proof boundary and assumptions.

For any constitutionally accepted scope, all applicable QS, PR, and RS obligations must be identified and discharged before full constitutional conformance may be claimed for that scope.

A single mathematical theorem or proof artifact may discharge more than one constitutional obligation. Such proof reuse does not merge, remove, or diminish the separate constitutional status of QS, PR, or RS.

New syntax, types, semantic objects, providers, targets, optimizations, implementations, or other capabilities cannot obtain exemption merely because they were absent when this Constitution was ratified.

Additional Fundamental-Theorem obligations may be adopted through constitutional amendment. The three obligations established here may not be removed.

## Article II — Edition Immutability

The ratified constitutional text shall remain unchanged within one Qleisli edition, except for mechanically generated metadata explicitly excluded from that text at ratification and the strictly pre-v1 rectification procedure authorized by Article IX.

The immutable text defines the permanent proof obligations and their governing rules. Binding interpretations, current formal realizations, the guarantee ledger, implementation details, proof artifacts, and governance records are maintained separately under those rules.

Updating such subordinate artifacts in accordance with this Constitution is permitted within the same edition.

Neither a new Lean declaration, a changed theorem statement, a changed formal representation, nor a changed implementation dependency graph is, by itself, a constitutional amendment.

## Article III — Constitutional Amendment

Except for a rectification validly authorized under Article IX before v1.0.0, changing the ratified constitutional text requires an explicit new edition, a separately reviewable constitutional diff, and a recorded transition.

A release-version change alone does not authorize constitutional amendment. A new edition is necessary for amendment but is not sufficient to override Article IV.

The following are distinct acts:

- **same-edition extension**: an admitted language or implementation capability grows while remaining subject to the existing constitutional obligations;
- **constitutional interpretation**: the Constitution Guardian Office determines the binding mathematical meaning or application of an existing constitutional obligation for a defined case or scope;
- **formal discharge or strengthening**: proofs or checked evidence establish, generalize, or strengthen the resulting obligations;
- **constitutional amendment**: the constitutional text changes through an edition transition.

A correction procedure may not serve as an unrecorded amendment.

Corrections to subordinate interpretations, records, formalizations, or proofs may be made under the ordinary rules of this Constitution, subject to Article IV and the guarantee ledger.

Correction of the ratified constitutional text itself is permitted only through Article IX before v1.0.0. Any proposed change that is an amendment rather than a rectification remains governed by this Article and Article IV.

## Article IV — Constitutional Continuity and Non-Weakening

The set of Fundamental-Theorem obligations may grow through constitutional amendment. It may not shrink.

No constitutionally continuous edition, amendment, release, implementation, interpretation, or correction may:

- remove an adopted Fundamental-Theorem obligation;
- merge an obligation away so that its separate constitutional status disappears;
- waive the requirement that an applicable obligation be discharged;
- render an obligation vacuous merely by definition;
- evade an obligation by renaming, rerouting, reclassifying, or moving an accepted capability outside an artificially narrowed formal boundary; or
- withdraw or weaken a guarantee already protected under Article VI; or
- transfer, delegate, proxy, automate, or otherwise vest any constitutional authority of the Constitution Guardian Office in an artificial intelligence, autonomous agent, software system, or analogous non-human computational entity.

Because this Constitution does not itself define the mathematical content of QS, PR, or RS, constitutional continuity does not require one global theorem statement to remain unchanged forever.

Instead, continuity protects the permanent existence of the obligations together with all binding guarantees already accumulated for their recorded scopes.

A later interpretation may introduce new mathematics, stronger formulations, broader domains, different representations, or new proof techniques. It may not use that freedom to invalidate, silently narrow, or weaken an existing protected guarantee over the scope to which that guarantee applies.

Discharged guarantees recorded under Article VI form a minimum that later releases must preserve within the edition.

An edition transition may not erase historical guarantees or be used to claim constitutional continuity for their weakening. Historical guarantees remain attached to their recorded domain, premises, and meaning; this does not assert that every future language has the same accepted programs.

The amendment and non-weakening protections themselves may not be repealed or weakened to authorize what this Article prohibits.

A development that abandons these protections is not constitutionally continuous with Qleisli under this Constitution.

## Article V — The Constitution Guardian Office

Qleisli shall maintain a **Constitution Guardian Office**.

The Constitution Guardian Office has final and exclusive constitutional authority to issue binding interpretations of the QS, PR, and RS obligations and to determine how this Constitution applies to new features and constitutional disputes.

This authority includes determining:

- which constitutional obligations apply to a case;
- the binding mathematical meaning required of those obligations for that case or scope;
- whether a proposed formal statement adequately represents the constitutional obligation;
- the relationship of a new obligation to existing binding interpretations and ledgered guarantees; and
- whether a proposed change is interpretation, formalization, extension, rectification, or constitutional amendment.

A binding interpretation must identify the case or scope, the applicable constitutional obligation or obligations, the required proposition or evidence sufficiently to make verification possible, its relationship to existing guarantees, and the reasons the ruling remains within the existing Constitution.

No issue, RFC, implementation, compiler behavior, documentation page, theorem name, Lean declaration, test, release note, or other subordinate artifact acquires authority to define the constitutional meaning of QS, PR, or RS merely by asserting or implementing such a meaning.

Only a binding interpretation issued under this Article and recorded under Article VI has such constitutional authority.

The Constitution Guardian Office determines **what must be proved**.

It does not determine mathematical truth by decree.

Mathematical derivability is established by Lean or the designated independent proof checker under the recorded proof boundary and assumptions. A ruling of the Constitution Guardian Office is therefore not itself a mathematical proof.

The Constitution Guardian Office may require constitutional review and suspend a proposed merge or release on a reasoned constitutional ground.

It has no general governing or veto authority over ordinary language design, implementation, libraries, tooling, releases, or project management that remain within the constitutional boundary.

The Constitution Guardian Office may not waive a Fundamental-Theorem obligation, deem a failed proof or check successful, withdraw a protected guarantee, redefine an obligation solely to evade an existing guarantee, or amend the Constitution by interpretation.

The current holder or holders, succession, and possible council form belong in governance documentation.

**Every holder, member, or other person exercising the constitutional authority of the Constitution Guardian Office must be a natural human person.**

An artificial intelligence, autonomous agent, language model, software system, algorithmic governance process, or analogous non-human computational entity may not:

- hold membership or office in the Constitution Guardian Office;
- exercise or share its binding interpretive authority;
- cast or determine a vote of the Office;
- issue, approve, or automatically adopt a binding interpretation;
- exercise its suspension or rectification authority;
- act as a proxy, delegate, substitute, or successor for a human holder; or
- acquire constitutional authority through automation, default rules, preauthorization, delegation, or failure of a human holder to intervene.

Artificial intelligence and other computational systems may be used as advisory tools for research, drafting, criticism, formalization, search, or analysis. Such assistance has no constitutional force by itself. Any resulting constitutional judgment must be knowingly reviewed and affirmatively adopted by the responsible human holder or human members of the Constitution Guardian Office as their own judgment.

A human holder may not evade this requirement by nominally retaining office while delegating the substance of constitutional judgment to a non-human system. Human review and responsibility must be substantive rather than ceremonial.

The Constitution Guardian Office may be occupied by one natural human person or by multiple natural human persons organized as a governance body defined by the governance documentation. Institutional succession must preserve the constitutional scope of the Constitution Guardian Office, its exclusively human authority, and the distinction between interpretation and mathematical verification.

## Article VI — Binding Interpretations and the Guarantee Ledger

A binding interpretation issued by the Constitution Guardian Office determines a constitutional obligation for its recorded case or scope. It has constitutional force only when affirmatively adopted by the human holder or human members authorized to act for the Office; no machine-generated or automatically generated interpretation becomes binding by default.

Before discharge, that interpretation is a binding pending obligation.

When its required proposition or evidence has been formally discharged and the result has been recorded, the discharged obligation enters the constitutional guarantee ledger as a protected guarantee.

A ledger entry identifies at least:

- the binding interpretation;
- the relevant QS, PR, or RS obligation or obligations;
- the language fragment, realization scope, accepted artifacts, and applicable premises;
- the mathematical property or evidence required by the interpretation;
- the semantic definitions or other formal objects needed to preserve its meaning;
- the proof or evidence binding; and
- its discharge status.

A pending obligation is not a discharged guarantee.

An ordinary helper lemma, implementation choice, experimental theorem, issue statement, or proof artifact does not enter the ledger merely because it exists or has been proved.

One proof may discharge several obligations or ledger entries when the correspondence is explicit and mechanically justified.

Within an edition, discharged ledger guarantees accumulate.

Later interpretations may clarify, generalize, or strengthen them, but may not repeal, relax, contradict, or silently narrow their recorded protection over their applicable scope.

Formal representations, theorem statements, semantic models, proof architectures, or implementations may be refactored or replaced. Such changes must preserve every applicable ledgered guarantee, with checked semantic transport where representations differ.

A binding interpretation that has not yet produced a discharged guarantee may be corrected or superseded by the Constitution Guardian Office with recorded reasons, provided that the change does not evade an applicable constitutional obligation or weaken any already protected guarantee.

Every later release must discharge all applicable ledger obligations against its current production acceptance machinery and current accepted artifacts.

Merely retaining an old proof about an old checker, old semantics, or old implementation does not satisfy this duty.

The ledger is a minimum accumulated guarantee. It is not an exhaustive definition of QS, PR, or RS.

Capabilities absent from historical ledger entries remain subject to all applicable Fundamental-Theorem obligations under Article I.

## Article VII — Production Acceptance and Mechanical Enforcement

The acceptance judgments appearing in constitutional proofs must correspond to the acceptance decisions actually used by the released implementation.

No narrower reference-only predicate, unchecked producer assertion, compatibility route, provider, backend, export path, or alternative implementation path may confer constitutional acceptance outside the proved boundary.

Each release must connect its accepted representations, semantics, realizations, resource evidence where applicable, and other constitutionally relevant artifacts to that boundary.

Historical obligations must remain connected to the behavior and evidence of the current accepted implementation through justified embeddings or semantic transport.

Release checks must enforce constitutional-text identity, ledger continuity, production-acceptance coverage, and the required proof or evidence verification.

They must distinguish the institutional judgment that a formal proposition adequately expresses a constitutional obligation from the mathematical judgment that the proposition is proved.

Reducing the set of accepted programs does not by itself weaken a safety implication.

Source compatibility, accepted-language continuity, and continued toolchain support are governed separately by published compatibility policy and conformance or regression checks. Those policies cannot justify accepting programs outside the constitutionally verified boundary.

## Article VIII — Ratification and Proof Maturity

This Constitution takes effect when the natural-human project maintainer affirmatively ratifies its exact text for Qleisli edition 2026 through an explicit ratification record. The record identifies the adopted text, the adopting human authority, and the effective date. Ratification precedes completion and publication of v0.3.0. That release verifies the identity of the ratified text and the applicable release conditions; it does not repeat ratification. Earlier drafts may be revised without creating a new edition.

Ratification permanently establishes QS, PR, and RS as the three Fundamental-Theorem obligations of edition 2026 together with the constitutional process by which they are interpreted, proved, and preserved.

Ratification does **not** elevate any then-current issue text, implementation convention, theorem statement, or working definition of QS, PR, or RS into constitutional text.

Nor does ratification assert that complete production proofs for all three obligations already exist in v0.3.0.

Before v1.0.0, releases must publish the binding interpretations applicable to their claimed scope, their proof coverage, provisional or incomplete formalizations, and outstanding obligations.

A pre-v1 capability lacking completed required coverage must not be represented as constitutionally certified for that scope.

Already discharged ledger guarantees remain binding; pre-v1 status does not authorize their withdrawal.

From v1.0.0 onward, every release claiming full constitutional conformance must discharge all applicable QS, PR, and RS obligations over the full scope of its accepted language and accepted realizations under the binding interpretations then governing that scope, and must preserve the accumulated guarantee ledger.

v1.0.0 therefore establishes full discharge as a release requirement.

It does not freeze one acceptance relation, one top-level Lean theorem type, one semantic representation, or one permanent global mathematical definition of QS, PR, or RS.

Later same-edition extensions may require new binding interpretations and stronger or different formal realizations under the same permanent Fundamental-Theorem obligations, subject always to Article IV and the accumulated ledger.

## Article IX — Pre-v1 Constitutional Rectification

Before the release of Qleisli v1.0.0, and only before that release, the Constitution Guardian Office may authorize correction of the ratified constitutional text when a defect is discovered in its logic, semantics, formalization, internal consistency, or textual expression.

The Constitution Guardian Office may authorize such a change only through its human holder or human members, and only if they determine, with recorded reasons, that the change is a **rectification rather than an amendment**. No artificial intelligence, autonomous agent, software system, or analogous non-human computational entity may exercise or be delegated this authority.

A rectification repairs the expression of an already adopted constitutional principle.

It must not introduce a new constitutional policy, Fundamental-Theorem obligation, guarantee, or proof obligation except insofar as logically necessary to make an already adopted principle coherent and correctly expressed.

Every constitutional rectification must publicly record at least:

- the previous text and the proposed corrected text;
- the defect in the previous text;
- why the previous text fails to express the already adopted constitutional principle;
- why the change is a rectification rather than an amendment;
- the constitutional and proof-theoretic consequences of the change; and
- the evidence that the change preserves Article IV and all applicable ledgered guarantees.

This authority may not be used to weaken or evade Article IV, withdraw a discharged guarantee, expand the Constitution Guardian Office's own constitutional jurisdiction, redefine a Fundamental-Theorem obligation merely to avoid an existing obligation, or replace an existing constitutional policy choice merely because a different design is later preferred.

The authority granted by this Article expires permanently upon the release of Qleisli v1.0.0.

After that release, any change to the ratified constitutional text requires an edition transition under Article III and remains subject to Article IV.
