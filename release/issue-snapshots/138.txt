## Status

Constitutional governance issue for **Qleisli 0.3.0**.

Related: #129 (Draft 5, Articles I/III/IV), #135 (guarantee ledger), #139 (jurisdictions), #149 (Guardian).

## Problem

Adding a Fundamental Theorem adds an enduring **constitutional proof jurisdiction**. It is not merely promoting a particular Lean proposition or helper lemma.

Article IV makes the set of such jurisdictions monotone across constitutionally continuous editions. Admission is therefore an irreversible constitutional act and needs explicit review.

## Admission requirements

A candidate additional jurisdiction should:

- protect a clear language-level or user-visible guarantee;
- have a purpose that survives implementation and representation replacement;
- explain why the existing Soundness, PR and RS jurisdictions do not already cover the duty;
- explain how it applies to current admitted behavior and future extensions;
- state its relationship to the Guardian Office, current production acceptance, and ledger;
- identify relevant assumptions and the semantic concepts needed for current formalization;
- include credible formal evidence for its current application and an explicit proof-coverage assessment;
- be adopted only by a recorded constitutional amendment and edition transition.

The exact theorem syntax and transitive Lean dependency graph must not be entrenched as the jurisdiction itself.

## Jurisdiction admission versus ledger discharge

These are different acts:

1. Adding a **new Fundamental-Theorem jurisdiction** changes the constitutional text and requires an edition transition.
2. Applying an **existing jurisdiction** to a feature is Guardian interpretation and can occur within the edition.
3. Proving and recording that binding interpretation adds a **ledgered guarantee** without adding a new Fundamental Theorem.
4. Proving a helper lemma alone performs none of these constitutional acts.

The three jurisdictions in edition 2026 are established at ratification even before all production proofs are complete. That initial proof-maturity schedule is governed by #129 Article VIII; it does not make Resource Safety provisional or absent.

For a later jurisdiction proposed after v1, the full-conformance release must discharge it for the current admitted scope before claiming conformance. A complete future-proof theorem statement covering unknown syntax is neither required nor possible as an admission mechanism.

## Questions

- What review record demonstrates that a genuinely new jurisdiction is needed?
- How are its protected purpose, scope and assumptions stated without freezing implementation-era syntax?
- Which current proofs/evidence and coverage report accompany adoption?
- How does it interact with existing ledgered guarantees without weakening them?
- How is constitutional adoption distinguished from an ordinary derived theorem?

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Require a standalone constitutional amendment and edition transition.
- [x] Preserve existing jurisdictions and guarantees under Article IV.
- [x] Require current formalization, semantic assumptions and proof-coverage evidence.
- [x] Distinguish jurisdiction adoption, binding interpretation, ledger discharge and helper lemmas.
- [x] Do not accidentally constitutionalize one implementation or theorem spelling.
- [x] Preserve the initial three-jurisdiction ratification and proof-maturity schedule.
- [x] Require complete applicable proof coverage for any post-v1 full-conformance claim.

## Core principle

> **Fundamental-Theorem status is scarce because its jurisdiction is permanent; proofs within that jurisdiction may continue to grow.**


## Acceptance audit and completion — 2026-10-05

All eight original criteria are satisfied as **rules for admission**, not as the addition or proof of a fourth jurisdiction. A separate read-only audit and integration review checked each criterion against the actual adopted artifacts, rather than treating existing checkboxes or the plan's adoption as evidence.

1. **Eligibility:** the [normative authority Reference](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/blob/10553a9f94f6fd6a4555961244e0e9be41154f8f/docs/src/reference/authority.md#conflicts-and-corrections) requires a distinct enduring purpose, relationship to existing duties, relevant assumptions and current coverage; it directly incorporates this Issue's full admission requirements.
2. **Amendment and edition transition:** [Constitution Articles I and III](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/blob/10553a9f94f6fd6a4555961244e0e9be41154f8f/CONSTITUTION.md) require a separately reviewable constitutional amendment and recorded new-edition transition for an additional Fundamental-Theorem obligation.
3. **Non-weakening:** Article IV preserves all established jurisdictions and ledgered guarantees across that transition.
4. **Current formalization and evidence:** the Reference's admission rule and the detailed requirements above require current semantic assumptions, formal evidence and proof-coverage assessment; theorem syntax and an implementation dependency graph are not the entrenched jurisdiction.
5. **Distinct acts:** Article III, Article VI and the Reference's interpretation/obligation/discharge section separate jurisdiction amendment, binding human interpretation, formal ledger discharge and ordinary helper lemmas.
6. **Representation independence:** the Preamble and Articles I–IV explicitly permit changing formalizations without constitutionalizing one theorem spelling or implementation.
7. **Initial maturity:** Articles I and VIII establish all three jurisdictions at ratification, distinguish pending from discharged obligations and preserve the pre-v1 disclosure schedule.
8. **Post-v1 conformance:** Article VIII and the Reference's proof-status section require full applicable current coverage before a full-conformance claim; admitting a later jurisdiction cannot evade its admission requirements or that coverage condition.
