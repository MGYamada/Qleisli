## Summary

Establish a constitutional rule for **Qleisli edition 2026**:

> **ε-approximation is not a constitutional semantic primitive and may not weaken any Fundamental-Theorem obligation.**

Qleisli's constitutional semantics should remain exact.

In particular, no edition-2026 guarantee may replace an exact obligation such as

```text
C realizes ⟦P⟧
```

with a weaker statement of the form

```text
distance(C, ⟦P⟧) ≤ ε.
```

Approximation may exist as an optional realization/export technique outside the constitutional acceptance contract, but **ε must not become part of the constitutional meaning of an accepted Qleisli program**.

The governing principle is:

> **Approximation is a realization technique, not a constitutional semantic primitive.**

---

## Motivation

Approximation looks locally convenient but becomes constitutional debt immediately if admitted into the semantic kernel.

Once a theorem is parameterized by an approximation budget, the Constitution must also decide or inherit answers to questions such as:

- which metric or norm is authoritative;
- how ε composes;
- whether global phase is quotiented out;
- how controlled operations transport the error;
- what the corresponding notion is for channels and instruments;
- whether approximation may justify ancilla cleanup;
- whether ε belongs to PR, RS, the source language, the backend, or the target profile;
- who chooses ε;
- whether changing ε changes program meaning;
- whether target resource guarantees quantify over ε.

Those questions are legitimate engineering and realization questions.

They should **not become constitutional definitions of quantum meaning**.

Edition 2026 should instead keep the constitutional core exact and push any future approximation mechanism to a strictly subordinate layer.

---

## Constitutional rule

For edition 2026, the following are prohibited as constitutional weakening mechanisms.

### 1. No ε-relaxed semantic equality

The meaning of a program must not be defined by tolerance:

```text
Meaning(P) ≈_ε U
```

as a substitute for an exact semantic object.

Floating-point or tolerance-based equality must not define semantic identity.

### 2. No ε-relaxed Qleisli Soundness

Soundness may not discharge an ownership, effect, phase, cleanup, or semantic obligation by proving that the violation is merely small.

Examples of forbidden substitutions include:

```text
Pr[dirty ancilla] ≤ ε
```

in place of exact cleanup, or

```text
‖U_checked - U_meaning‖ ≤ ε
```

in place of the exact semantic promise made by the accepted object.

### 3. No ε-relaxed Physical Realizability

The constitutional PR jurisdiction must remain a checked **exact correspondence** obligation for whatever realization is admitted as constitutionally realized.

Conceptually:

```text
Accepted(P)
∧ AdmittedRealizationρ(P, C, E)
→ Realizesρ(C, ⟦P⟧, E).
```

It must not silently become:

```text
→ Dist(⟦C⟧, ⟦P⟧) ≤ ε.
```

If a backend emits an approximate circuit, that artifact is not thereby a constitutionally exact realization of the source meaning.

### 4. No ε-relaxed Resource Safety

RS must not acquire an approximation budget merely because a synthesis algorithm has one.

A target cost depending on ε may be useful engineering information, but it must not redefine the constitutional resource obligation for edition 2026.

### 5. No ε as the bridge between gate paradigms

A program that is not exactly representable in one gate/coefficient paradigm must not be made constitutionally valid in that paradigm by introducing a tolerance.

The allowed architectural responses are:

- select a richer exact paradigm;
- extend the coefficient domain;
- use scalar/base extension;
- reject the exact realization under that paradigm;
- or produce a non-constitutional approximate backend artifact.

This rule is especially important for the progression:

```text
Clifford+T
    ↓
Clifford+cyclotomic
    ↓
ℂ
```

The mathematical universe should be enlarged explicitly rather than patched by an ε escape hatch.

## Relationship to paradigms

This issue is the constitutional counterpart of #309 and #310.

#309 removes Clifford+T as a hidden exactness assumption by moving to the single exact cyclotomic coefficient domain `R_∞ = ℤ[ζ_{2^∞}, 1/2]`.

#310 introduces **paradigm** as an axis independent of constitutional edition.

Together they provide the clean alternative to ε-based semantics:

```text
exact program meaning
        ×
constitutional edition
        ×
gate-set paradigm
        ×
realization profile
```

If the exact meaning cannot be realized under one paradigm, Qleisli changes the realization domain or reports failure.

It does not change the truth condition by inserting ε.

---

## Orthogonality to NISQ / FTQC

This rule must not encode:

```text
NISQ = approximate
FTQC = exact
```

The NISQ/FTQC boundary is independent of the constitutional exactness rule.

A NISQ-oriented realization may still be modeled against an exact mathematical semantic object.

An FTQC backend may still use practical approximate synthesis internally.

Neither fact requires approximation to become part of the constitutional semantics.

---

## Numerical implementations are not forbidden

This issue does **not** prohibit numerical computation, floating-point acceleration, verified numerics, interval methods, or approximate internal algorithms.

The boundary is constitutional.

A numerical procedure may assist the implementation if the final accepted claim is still an exact proposition justified by checked evidence.

For example:

```text
numerical / interval / approximate computation
        ↓
checked certificate
        ↓
exact constitutional proposition
```

is compatible with this issue.

What is prohibited is making the tolerance itself the final constitutional guarantee.

This distinction is important for the future ℂ paradigm in #310: a high-performance Lean representation may use sophisticated numerical techniques internally, but the Constitution must not collapse mathematical equality or realizability into an arbitrary ε-threshold.

## Impact on existing approximation issues

### #31

#31 correctly established the essential rule that **source meanings are exact** and approximation belongs to a separate synthesis/backend contract.

This issue strengthens that boundary constitutionally.

For edition 2026, any language in #31 suggesting that certified approximate target programs discharge constitutional PR should be interpreted more narrowly:

- approximate backend artifacts may exist;
- they may carry metrics, error bounds, and certificates;
- but the ε-bound itself does not constitute edition-2026 PR.

### #163

#163 describes resource certification after an approximation budget is closed.

Under this constitutional rule, such certification is **non-constitutional target/tooling certification** unless and until a future constitutional edition explicitly admits a corresponding approximation jurisdiction.

It must not be confused with edition-2026 Resource Safety.

No existing approximation issue may silently widen the constitutional theorem jurisdictions.

## Future editions

This issue governs edition 2026.

A future edition may deliberately introduce a constitutional approximation concept, but that must be an explicit constitutional change with a fully specified mathematical contract.

It may not enter by implementation drift, backend convenience, or reinterpretation of an existing theorem.

Any such future proposal would need to settle, at minimum:

- metric;
- phase convention;
- composition;
- channel/instrument semantics;
- cleanup interaction;
- control interaction;
- resource interaction;
- acceptance identity;
- theorem-jurisdiction impact.

Until then, edition 2026 remains exact.

## Required constitutional consequences

- **Soundness:** exact semantic and structural obligations remain exact.
- **Physical Realizability:** constitutionally admitted realization remains exact correspondence.
- **Resource Safety:** no ε-dependent weakening of the constitutional resource jurisdiction.
- **Ancilla cleanliness:** approximate cleanliness never authorizes exact release.
- **Controlled semantics:** approximate phase agreement never replaces exact phase obligations.
- **Paradigm selection:** non-membership in an exact gate/coefficient domain is not repaired constitutionally by tolerance.
- **Edition compatibility:** adding approximation to the constitutional contract requires an explicit edition-level decision.

## Non-goals

- Do not ban approximate compilation as a backend feature.
- Do not ban numerical algorithms inside the implementation.
- Do not ban floating-point execution where it is outside semantic identity.
- Do not choose a universal approximation metric.
- Do not define ε-composition rules for edition 2026 constitutional semantics.
- Do not identify NISQ with approximation.
- Do not identify FTQC with exact Clifford+T.
- Do not require every physical execution to be mathematically noiseless.
- Do not make a practical hardware noise model part of the constitutional semantic kernel.

---

## Core principle

> **Qleisli may approximate an implementation. It must not approximate its Constitution.**

Equivalently:

> **Exact meaning first. Gate-set variation is handled by paradigms. Approximation, if used, remains subordinate realization machinery.**
