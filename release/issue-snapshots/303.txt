## Summary

Qleisli 0.3.0 should make the interaction between `ctrl` coherent-control access and exclusive `excl` quantum access precise enough to prove a local **commutation theorem** for compatible accesses.

This is the theorem-level counterpart of the intended access discipline in:

- #29 — exclusive temporary quantum `excl` access
- #69 — basis-preserving coherent `ctrl` access
- #85 — evaluation order, temporaries, and quantum lifetime boundaries
- #20 — symmetric authoring for diagonal phase operations

The goal is not to make source evaluation order unspecified. Source semantics should remain deterministic. The goal is to identify a mechanically checkable condition under which a compiler or proof may safely exchange two already well-typed quantum operations.

The syntax migration does not alter the theorem shape: `excl` supplies the exclusive arbitrary-access footprint and `ctrl` supplies the basis-preserving shared footprint.

## Motivation

Rust obtains race freedom from an aliasing discipline:

> aliasing XOR mutability

Qleisli has a quantum analogue.

Arbitrary quantum mutation is exclusive through `excl`. Overlap may remain possible through `ctrl`, but `ctrl` is restricted to computational-basis-preserving coherent access.

This suggests a local theorem of the form:

> **overlap only through compatible `ctrl` access implies commutation.**

If this theorem holds under the exact Qleisli access contracts, the language gains a strong semantic result directly from its type/access system:

- safe reordering of independent quantum operations;
- evaluation-order independence for specific typed fragments;
- a proof basis for scheduling and optimization;
- a precise explanation of why QFT controlled-phase layers admit reordering;
- a theorem-level justification for the `ctrl` / `excl` split.

This should be treated as a Soundness-local lemma, not merely an optimization heuristic.

## Candidate theorem shape

For operations `U` and `V`, distinguish:

- `M_U`, `M_V`: axes/subsystems accessed through arbitrary exclusive `excl`;
- `C_U`, `C_V`: axes/subsystems accessed only through `ctrl`.

A candidate compatibility condition is:

```text
M_U ∩ (M_V ∪ C_V) = ∅
M_V ∩ (M_U ∪ C_U) = ∅
```

so that any overlap is confined to

```text
C_U ∩ C_V
```

and both operations are basis-preserving on every shared controlled subsystem.

Under the authoritative semantics, prove the corresponding commutation result, conceptually:

```text
U ; V  ≡  V ; U
```

with the exact equality notion chosen by the language semantics.

The final theorem statement may differ from this schematic form. The important requirement is that the proof be derived from the actual access contracts, not postulated as an optimizer rule.

## Proof idea to validate

On the union of shared `ctrl` subsystems, decompose in the computational basis.

Because each admitted `ctrl` use is block diagonal in those basis projectors, both operations decompose into control sectors.

Inside each fixed sector, the non-control quantum actions are on disjoint mutable subsystems by the compatibility conditions above. Those sectorwise actions therefore commute.

The proof must account explicitly for:

- multiple shared controlled axes;
- entanglement with arbitrary external reference systems;
- phase kickback;
- owner ordering / tensor-factor bookkeeping;
- exact semantics of nested or composite `ctrl` uses;
- any zero-width / unit cases;
- the equality notion used by Qleisli.

## QFT consequence

The current textbook-shaped example in #29 uses:

```qli
controlled_phase[1, k-j+1](ctrl q[k], ctrl q[j]);
```

but the diagonal controlled-phase operation should be reviewed against #69.

If both operands need only basis-preserving coherent access, the example should use the final `ctrl` spelling instead of arbitrary `excl` access for those operands.

Then the inner QFT phase layer becomes a canonical example of this theorem: the typed access contract itself explains why compatible diagonal phase operations may be reordered.

This issue should update #29/#69 examples as necessary once the exact rule is adopted.

## Relationship to evaluation order

This issue must **not** make general quantum expression evaluation unordered.

#85 should continue to define deterministic source evaluation.

Instead:

- source order remains normative;
- arbitrary optimizations may not reorder noncommuting operations;
- a compiler may exchange operations only when a proved semantics-preserving rule applies;
- this commutation theorem may provide one such rule.

## Design principle

> **Qleisli should turn its quantum access discipline into a theorem: overlap through coherent control may be shareable, arbitrary quantum mutation may not be, and compatible typed accesses should carry their commutation proof with them.**
