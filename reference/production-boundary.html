Qleisli Programming Language

Current production verification boundary

The production verification boundary is the set of entry paths that reach the native checker, together with what each check establishes. The native checker is the Lean executable that lean-kernel/ builds. A mode is one dispatch argument of the native checker, such as --runtime-root-check. An entry path is a Rust API or a CLI command that submits bytes to a mode.

Read this chapter to find out what a successful check proves and what it leaves open. For each mode, the chapter states four things: the request that the mode reads, the rules that the native checker applies, the Lean theorems that bind the check, and the obligations that no theorem discharges. The section on a mode lists the theorems under the label “Proof scope” and the open obligations under the label “Not established”.

The chapter is subordinate to three documents: the authority hierarchy, the adopted single-verifier decision that History cites, and repository TRUSTBOUNDARY.md. It does not discharge a constitutional obligation or widen an existing theorem. Source paths identify files in the Qleisli repository.

Production acceptance has one implementation, the native checker. Rust does the work around the check and never accepts a program itself.

Rules:

  • Rust generates proposals, and it selects and invokes the compatible checker. It retains the checked bytes, which are immutable. It also decodes views, reports diagnostics and executes simulations.
  • A missing, incompatible or failing native checker rejects. No other verifier takes its place: the retired Rust verifier and the dual-acceptance fallback are absent.
  • Invoking Lean and granting a semantic accepted handle are different things. Not every public function does either. Public operations that confer no acceptance lists the operations that produce no acceptance. One of them invokes Lean all the same: QIRF export has the native checker inspect its output. That check establishes portable validity. It is neither a fresh execution acceptance nor a translation theorem.

The accepted judgment of a mode is the success of its Lean check on the submitted bytes. Entrypoints and actual checked representations identifies the judgment and the checked representation of each mode. Two scoped guarantees are admitted. They concern ownership and classical scope of the original decoded ordinary root.

Under the adopted interpretations, the three obligations concern these subjects:

ObligationSubject
QSThe declared typing, ownership, effect and exact semantic contracts of the actual accepted object
PRForward correspondence from that meaning to the actual admitted target artifact, under its target and workspace premises
RSFinite, checked bounds for the accepted behavior and the applicable realization, under the stated resource model

Their broader proof obligations and the strict exactness obligations are pending.

Rules:

  • Elaboration must preserve the specified source meaning for every claimed source scope. Its evidence links the original source and dependency contracts to the checked artifact, including its owners, ordered axes, effects and exact phase.
  • A successful check of an artifact establishes the properties of that artifact that the check covers. It does not establish that the artifact corresponds to its source. A preservation theorem or independent validation must establish the source correspondence separately.
  • The same rule applies to an adapter that claims to preserve a foreign input, and to a backend that claims preservation in the output it emits.
  • Source syntax that a later change adds may elaborate under the same edition whenever it preserves the adopted contracts.
  • A checked representation that replaces another must carry the applicable current guarantees with it, through justified semantic transport.
  • Every frontend and backend that claims constitutional acceptance must use the applicable native gate and its current evidence.
  • Missing evidence or a failed check rejects the claimed scope.

Ordinary QIRF root requests

The mode --qirf-native checks an ordinary QIRF root. Checking or compiling a source, a project or a qrate reaches this mode. The first row of the entry-path table lists its entry paths and its bytes. The bytes are a QLV3 envelope. The envelope holds the original QLV1 packet, with its optional independent request. When the caller requests execution, it also holds the retained execution view.

Rules:

  • The accepted judgment is the success of QleisliKernel.Protocol.ExecutionView.Native.ordinary on the QLV3 payload at its requested scope. The checker reaches that function through Protocol.NativeContext.run.
  • Execution requires one more check: a comparison of the retained view against the view that Lean decodes.

Proof scope:

  • ordinary_original binds the accepted judgment to Protocol.Validity.Acceptance for the original QLV1 packet. That witness identifies the decoded Qirf.Artifact, the order and the optional independent request. It also identifies the success of Qirf.Validity.inspect on those exact values.
  • Qleisli.NativeValidity.check_sound binds success to VerifiedMeaning.
  • check_scopeSafe covers classical scope.

Not established:

  • Full ordinary EffectSound/CPTP is a separate obligation.
  • Preservation by source translation and preservation by foreign translation are separate obligations.
  • Preservation in emitted output and preservation at runtime are separate obligations.

Native contract requests

A caller uses the mode --qirf-contract when it needs a fact about a QIRF root beyond ordinary validity: an encoded equation, an exact matrix, a control sector or an observing instrument. The mode reads the original QIRF root and, beside it, a qleisli.native-contract request of version 1. It decodes the request strictly. The field kind selects one of six request kinds:

encodedformat, version, kind, contractEncoded and square leaf

Rules:

  • The inner QLV1 format is the same for every kind. An external call requires the QLV3 envelope and its scope.
  • The control kind changes neither the inner QLV1 format nor the contracts of the encoded and leaf requests.

Proof scope:

  • The native-contract bridge is specific to Protocol.NativeContract.check. It does not inherit the theorem for the ordinary Protocol.Validity.check root.
  • Its Acceptance witness retains what the check read and decoded: the original QLV1 body and request bytes, the UTF-8/JSON decoding, the artifact and order, the requested signature, the ordered ports and the exact matrix or encoded contract. It retains them with continuous work states. These facts are necessary for success. They are not a converse characterization of every parser constraint.
  • Protocol.NativeContract.check_acceptance binds the original bytes, the decoded request and the actual checking states.
  • tests/fixtures/constitution_v030/native-contract-bridge/ preserves the unchanged executable prefix, the printed theorem and predicate types, the axiom sets and a validation record.

Not established:

  • The identity and proof checks of that fixture admit no guarantee and do not close S05.
  • They establish no claim about the source or the compiler, a deployed binary, Rust rematerialization, the target or the runtime, or quantitative Resource Safety.

Encoded and square-leaf requests

A request of kind encoded states an encoded equation for the root. A request of kind leaf states a signature, ordered ports and an exact square matrix for it. The entry-path table gives the entry paths and the checked bytes of the kinds encoded and leaf.

Rules:

  • The square kind: "leaf" protocol keeps its two-sided unitary conditions. It is available beside the rectangular kind.

Proof scope:

  • Qleisli.NativeContract.check_encoded_sound and encoded_meaning connect success to three things: the BodyMeaning of the original root, the exact Basis interface and the requested encoded equation. Through EncodedMeaning, the encoded branch retains that BodyMeaning, the complete Basis interface and the checked encoded equation.
  • Wrapper.circuitMatrix_entries proves that the coefficients of the actual wrapper equal those of the original matrix.
  • encoded_reference extends the equation to any reference amplitude function.
  • Qleisli.NativeContract.check_sound and leaf_meaning compose the native acceptance of the bytes to the instrument meaning of the original body. They cover the bounded closed unitary fragment, the exact requested matrix, the signature and the ordered ports. The output port is tied to an actual verification.
  • leaf_reference_laws extends both whole-space inverse laws to every finite reference. The leaf conclusion retains complex phase. Its laws for an arbitrary finite reference require no premise of separability or normalization.

Not established:

  • For the kind encoded, these claims are separate: source and compiler, compiled I/O, runtime and export, clean release and quantitative resources. The theorems are not ledger admissions.
  • For the kind leaf, source and runtime claims are separate. Broader claims about effects and clean release are separate too.
  • Both kinds give bounded results about the original root. These results do not establish correspondence with the source, with a deployed binary or with host execution.

Rectangular pure-leaf requests

A request of kind pure-leaf states that a unary pure function equals an exact matrix. The input type and the output type are independent, so the matrix can be rectangular. The mode --qirf-contract accepts qleisli.native-contract version 1 with kind: "pure-leaf". Its complete fields are format, version, kind, domain, codomain, input, output and matrix.

Rules:

  • Both types use exact QIRF basis trees. The ports retain token, ordered wires and bit shape.
  • The native checker rejects an unknown field, an unknown version and a producer acceptance flag.
  • The interface of the original QIRF root describes a unary pure function with independent input and output types. The structural check of that interface requires four things: a Unitary or Isometry effect, no classical ports, one input and one actual live output owner, and the corresponding input and output widths.
  • The reader of the literal original body finds the output owner. The input width does not stand for the output width.
  • Ordinary validity alone does not establish an external matrix equation.
  • PureLeaf.check freshly checks the original dependency graph and body. It binds both exact type trees and both ordered ports. It reconstructs every input column, and it checks exact equality to the requested matrix and V†V = I.
  • Reconstruction retains the original effect checks, complete classical branches and hidden observation histories. Measurement, reset and discard cannot be relabeled pure or erased from the equation.
  • Zero-width owners, exact phase and axis order are part of the obligation.
  • Both endpoint dimensions keep the six-bit dense bound and the shared exact-work accounting. The kind adds no unbounded matrix API.
  • Every receipt requires fresh native acceptance, including a receipt that uses previously checked dependency data.

Implementation note. CheckedPureLeaf is a receipt that only private code can construct. It covers the immutable bytes of the original artifact and of the request. On the Rust side, boundary construction and matrix serialization validate only the shape of untrusted data.

Implementation note. The ordinary frontend retains SourcePureEquation sidecars on its accepted entry. A sidecar holds an implementation leaf and a reference leaf, each independently checked. It also holds their exact matching domain, codomain and matrix, the resolved names and the original source text. Constructor lowering rechecks actual renamed Raw fragments against the derived matrix. Selected Raw lowering retains its original Meaning requests and checks actual instruction intervals. The refined finite-leaf path of the hierarchy is square and refuses rectangular Meaning obligations.

Proof scope:

  • pure_leaf_meaning binds the fresh check of the graph and the root to the original body and the exact Gram identity.
  • pure_leaf_reference preserves inner products for arbitrary joint reference amplitudes.

Not established:

  • The check grants neither inverse nor control access. It supplies no source or runtime correspondence, no target realization and no quantitative resource evidence.
  • Source snapshots provide provenance. Neither sidecar is a general proof of source preservation, a functional specification of an arbitrary whole entry or a constitutional guarantee.

Original-artifact control-sector requests

A control-sector check asks whether an action keeps the computational-basis sectors of the requested axes apart. The rules below state the exact condition. Each source ctrl call carries such a check as an obligation. The mode --qirf-contract accepts two kinds that request a control-sector check of an original artifact:

RequestComplete field setContent
kind: "control"format, version, kind, signature and axessignature is the exact QIRF basis tree. axes is an ordered list of distinct physical bit positions in that interface.
kind = "control-owners"format, version, kind, signatures and axessignatures is the requested basis forest of an original call with several ordered quantum ports.

Rules:

  • The native checker rejects a control request with an extra field. A proposed matrix is such a field, and so is a producer success flag.
  • A control-owners request carries exactly its five fields. It accepts neither a proposed matrix nor a success receipt.
  • Lean checks the original graph, the root and the dependency evidence, and it checks that the input and output interfaces are identical. It reconstructs the actual action and checks that the action is unitary on the whole space. It then requires every matrix entry between different requested computational-basis sectors to be exactly zero.
  • The six-bit finite reconstruction limit and continuous work accounting apply.
  • An empty axis list is meaningful for a zero-width logical owner. It does not erase ownership or nontrivial phase.
  • The internal Qirf.ControlAccess.checkOwners component checks an original call with several ordered quantum ports. It constructs no wrapper that packs them into a single port. The requested basis forest must match the widths and positions of the original input owners and of the live returned owners, including zero-width owners.
  • Raw bit widths do not establish source type trees. The requested forest requires binding to the original checked source.

Rules for source ctrl calls:

  • The Finite project path binds the control-owners request before it updates source bindings. It binds the request to each interval of an actual emitted control call, to the original ordered owner ports and basis forest, and to the returned tokens. It checks nested calls and unused concrete declarations. The complete final artifact also requires ordinary native acceptance.
  • Selected source projection retains the original ordered access roles, together with the source identities and positions of the call. This includes public ctrl occurrences. Elaboration checks the exact owner shape and actual effect. It neither grants sector authority nor rewrites the role to excl.
  • Checker-free Raw lowering and hierarchical lowering reject retained control roles.
  • Native-checked Selected Raw lowering independently checks the selected root and every retained concrete body that bears a control. It then replays its actual decoded calls with fresh sector requests. Final consumption repeats native acceptance and source replay. Retaining a role or an earlier success is not acceptance authority.
  • The common judgment records each original control call as a pending sector obligation. Native-checked Selected Raw lowering requires every original obligation to have a concrete interval in each retained specialization of its declaration. A program with an absent interval is unsupported. One specialization cannot cover the missing call of another.
  • Unused original declarations, unselected branches, empty loops and zero-count providers cannot erase the obligation or its location. Retained zero-count provider bodies are checked before root emission.
  • Control replay refuses when it has no native checker.
  • Symbolic inverse or controlled replay refuses retained control roles. Its transformed trace does not supply the original call intervals that the native bridge requires.
  • Zero-count and unused-provider obligations must be covered before selected transport can be admitted. Empty output does not waive them.

Implementation note. The internal Raw replay bridge can discharge a retained control obligation with the checker of the accepted original artifact. It selects the actual instruction interval that replay consumes, its input and returned tokens and its ordered source basis forest. It then submits a fresh control-owners request. It takes no call body, matrix or success receipt from a producer.

Work is shared across these decisions within the exact-work ceiling. The checked Raw entry shares this budget across all body and root decisions. It refuses an increased ceiling, exhaustion or an unavailable checker. Regression tests use original public control source graphs as well as private graph mutations.

Proof scope:

  • Protocol.NativeContract.ControlAcceptance and check_acceptance retain the original body and request bytes, the exact decoded signature and axes, and the actual checker execution.
  • Qleisli.NativeContract.control_meaning connects the same action to four things: the BodyMeaning of the original root, its exact interface, the sector condition and the inverse laws.
  • control_reference proves projector commutation on arbitrary joint complex amplitudes with any external reference. Phase kickback and entanglement are permitted. The result does not assert that the reduced control state is unchanged, and it does not assert separability.
  • checked_owners_meaning and checked_owners_action_project bind the actual root, its dependencies and the full complex action to the sector law.
  • ControlOwnersAcceptance retains the decoded ordered forest and coordinates, the original packet bytes and the actual checking execution.
  • control_owners_meaning and control_owners_reference extend the original-body result and the arbitrary-reference result to the control-owners request.

Not established:

  • The control request alone does not enable source ctrl calls.
  • These are separate obligations: original source places, the artifact identity that is specific to a call, aliasing, access lifetimes, lowering and execution correspondence, and quantitative resource and scheduling claims.
  • The two kinds introduce no native mode, external schema admission or constitutional guarantee.
  • Neither an annotation nor native unitarity alone supplies control-sector evidence.
  • Host source lowering and interval extraction are not a Lean source-preservation theorem.
  • AST-to-step preservation, Rust decoding and interval extraction are separate obligations. The Raw replay bridge is a host bridge, not a Lean source-preservation theorem.

  • The ordered quantum owners and the classical projection must match the original roots.
  • Source consumers select this contract by the Observe effects that callers see.
  • The native profile requires original artifacts that genuinely observe. A pure body can carry a conservative Observe bound. That bound neither rewrites the Raw classification of the body nor authorizes a fallback to a pure contract.
  • Unsupported concrete pairs refuse, including unused ones.
  • The native profile has one quantum input and no runtime classical inputs.
  • Signatures are requested type data. Native widths alone do not reconstruct the full types of the source or prove their preservation.

Root loading and compatibility during generic migration

This section states which sources a load checks, what provenance a receipt retains, and which compatibility duties a migration to generic rules keeps.

Rules for loading:

  • frontend::project::Project loads every .qli module beneath its selected source root and all bundled ordinary sources.
  • Ordinary project compilation checks the root of every materialized concrete ordinary function, including unused functions.
  • Classical bodies and Meaning declarations have their own obligations of preparation and checking, which are separate.
  • Common source checking covers every declaration, including unused declarations and both branch bodies.
  • QrateSource in src/frontend/project/manifest.rs provides opt-in selection of an explicitly declared [source].root. That selection chooses the source tree. It does not introduce reachable-only loading or exempt unused siblings inside the tree.
  • With an explicit module map, ParsedProgram::parse checks every supplied module and the bundled registry before it selects a concrete entry.

Rules for the provenance of receipts:

  • The function receipts and instrument receipts of the ordinary finite frontend retain source provenance separately from the loading policy. So do local equation sidecars.
  • Each retained declaration contributes its original resolved lexical dependencies, including type and Nat expressions, Meaning references, inactive branches and zero-count operations.
  • Actual bound Op descriptors and checked Raw receipt dependencies contribute their original source records as well.
  • Pure providers retain their original declaration and equation association. Instrument calls also retain the original caller and its concrete Op bindings for source-span binding.
  • The resulting closure retains complete original module text, including comments, and shares immutable module records across receipts. Unrelated loaded modules need not appear in each receipt.
  • The metadata limits and the aggregate native transport limits apply.
  • This selection changes neither loading nor eager checking of every loaded declaration. It does not implement a reachable-module policy.
  • These records provide provenance. They are not a source-preservation proof or acceptance authority.

Rules for a reachable-module policy, which this chapter does not enable:

  • Such a policy must specify entry roots, transitive imports, providers and Meaning dependencies, independent checking of the selected graph, bundled-library validation and the treatment of unused sources.
  • It must retain original module, root and dependency identities and reject stale, missing or replaced references.
  • It must preserve the permitted import-only cycles. Imports have no runtime action.
  • Forbidden dependency cycles through calls, Meanings and providers retain the rules of their actual profiles, including any explicitly admitted decreasing recursion.
  • Diagnostics and source-selection migration need review before a change of the all-source behavior.

Rules for compatibility:

  • The five-role architecture assigns compatibility duties for these: flat Raw IR, both QIRF profiles, finite contracts, five hierarchy modes, component protocols, the source, qrate and module-map entrypoints, the Rust and Python clients, foreign adapters and research comparisons.
  • The five hierarchy modes are --hierarchy-pending, --hierarchy-request-pending, --hierarchy-fourier-pending, --instrument-pending and --qpe-instrument-pending. The architecture records no compatibility duty for the sixth, --hierarchy-control-pending.
  • A later migration must preserve each supported contract, or state its explicit versioned replacement, its unsupported cases and its migration tests.
  • Replaced wrappers and their proofs must stay built and audited until their supported callers and obligations migrate.
  • Missing or incompatible native checking must reject through every route.
  • Compatibility does not promise to resurrect a retired API. frontend::sized moved to frontend::compile. The qleisli sized command prefix was removed, with explicit migration diagnostics.
  • The separate research and Python comparison readers do not restore that production namespace or supply alternative source acceptance.
  • Edition 2026 identifies the constitutional regime. Source, API and protocol compatibility is versioned separately.

  • Five things are distinct evidence: the unification of the implementation, publication, the compilation of proofs, tests and audits.
