## Goal

Implement constitutional CI that preserves the ratified text and accumulating guarantees while allowing current formalizations and implementations to evolve.

The former proposal to freeze theorem/schema roots and their normative dependency closure at v1.0.0 is superseded. Hashes may identify historical artifacts; they must not freeze future theorem syntax or replace interpretation and preservation proofs.

## Ratification-first bootstrap

Ratification is the first dedicated stage of the approved 0.3.0 work, before dependent language implementation. Prepare the exact constitutional text, a compact manifest, and a reviewable ratification record first. Until the natural-human project maintainer affirmatively adopts that exact text, record `awaiting human ratification`; a draft hash, an approved work plan, passing CI, or an AI-authored record is not ratification.

The initial adoption record identifies edition 2026, the adopting human authority, the exact text identity, and the effective date. Appointment of the Constitution Guardian Office is a separate recorded act; initial ratification does not depend on an already constituted Office.

After human ratification, bind the protected text identity to that event and enforce the same-edition text and Article IX rules immediately. Ratification does not discharge any proof obligation. The later 0.3.0 release gate verifies the recorded ratification and all applicable readiness checks; it is not the first ratification event.

The historical candidate-integrity checker validates the review packet only. The subsequent real ratification is recorded in #129, and a separate adopted-identity checker enforces that event, its approved artifacts and the archived bootstrap plus the active interpretation/pending-obligation ledger. Neither checker independently authenticates a human or completes this constitutional-enforcement issue. A valid Article IX correction would need its concrete human record and separately reviewed enforcement support; editing a hash cannot authorize it.

## Minimal constitutional manifest

Record, using a small maintainable format:

- active edition, ratification status, and the initial human adoption record identifying the adopting authority, exact text identity, and effective date;
- identity of the ratified constitutional text;
- binding Guardian interpretation records and pending/discharged status;
- ledger entry identities, protected contracts, and semantic dependencies;
- current production acceptance boundary and artifact binding;
- proof/evidence verification entrypoints and declared assumptions;
- justified transports from historical ledger representations where needed;
- release proof coverage and outstanding obligations.

Do not serialize the whole transitive Lean dependency graph as immutable constitutional law.

## Required checks

### Constitutional text

A same-edition change to ratified `CONSTITUTION.md` fails **unless** it is a valid pre-v1 rectification explicitly authorized and recorded under #129 Article IX. Otherwise, an explicit edition transition must record its constitutional diff and satisfy non-weakening review; incrementing a number does not waive Article IV.

### Guarantee ledger

Detect deletion, relaxation, or silent semantic redefinition of a discharged guarantee. Keep historical records auditable.

Allow appended interpretations and meaning-preserving proof/representation refactors, with checked transport or preservation evidence. A general proof may discharge multiple entries; a separate CI job for every entry is not required.

### Current production acceptance

Verify that the release gate and all certified program/realization paths are within the acceptance boundary used by the proofs. Track the actual emitted artifacts, resource evidence, target/profile contracts, and relevant checker identity.

A proof over an unused or narrower reference predicate is not sufficient. A review marker cannot authorize a bypass.

### Proof replay

For every release claiming full conformance, re-check:

1. the three current jurisdiction-wide obligations over the current admitted scope;
2. every applicable discharged historical ledger obligation against current acceptance and artifacts;
3. the semantic transport/preservation needed to connect old contracts to current behavior.

Existing proof terms and general preservation theorems may be reused. "Re-check" does not require reconstructing proofs manually.

Do not use only `NewTheorem -> OldTheorem` between closed proved propositions as a continuity check. The current acceptance and behavior must be part of the preservation obligation.

### Separate compatibility gate

Use corpus/QLT and compatibility policy to detect regressions in accepted-language continuity. Acceptance shrinkage is not, by itself, loss of a safety implication; it may still fail the separate release compatibility gate.

## 0.3.0 and v1.0.0

0.3.0 may introduce constitutional-text checks, interpretation/ledger records, boundary binding, and explicit proof-status reporting before all three production proofs are complete.

A pending obligation must never be marked discharged because a test passes or the Guardian approves a plan. Pre-v1 certification claims must state their verified scope.

From v1.0.0, full conformance requires complete current-scope and ledger discharge under #129 Article VIII. No theorem-statement freeze occurs at v1.

## Enforcement limits

CI verifies declared artifacts and formal proofs; the Guardian determines whether a formalization adequately addresses the constitutional case. Neither is a substitute for the other.

Make checks deterministic and runnable locally. Keep the implementation small and allow ordinary refactors that preserve the guarantees.

### Article IX rectification gate

Before v1.0.0 only, CI may admit a same-edition constitutional-text change solely when the protected record identifies it as a **Draft 5 Article IX rectification** authorized by the human Constitution Guardian Office, records the reasons it restores an already adopted principle rather than changing policy, and demonstrates that it does not weaken or evade any discharged guarantee.

No AI-generated or automatically generated authorization can satisfy this gate by itself.
