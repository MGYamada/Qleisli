Qleisli Programming Language

Surface-to-core elaboration

An elaboration is the expansion of one surface form of a .qli program into operations of an existing core representation. A surface form is a construct as the program writes it, such as a call, a pattern or a fold.

This chapter specifies the convenience elaborations that edition 2026 permits. It gives a contract for each one. A contract names the surface form and the target of the expansion. It states the evaluation order, the owners, the exact phase and the source spans. It lists the refusals, and it lists the obligations that the expansion leaves open. The chapter is subordinate to the authority hierarchy.

The chapter introduces no syntax, no primitive, no implicit conversion, no evidence authority and no supported emitter.

Rules:

  • An elaboration may insert only the uniquely determined operations that its section describes.
  • An elaboration must preserve five things: the evaluation order, the complete type trees, the logical owners, the exact phase and the original source attribution.
  • An elaboration must not infer an arbitrary isomorphism, a permutation, cleanup, operator equality or an unavailable capability.

Source and acceptance boundary

This section names the check that comes before elaboration and the check that decides acceptance after it.

The common checker applies the common source judgment to the complete original AST of a program. Elaboration comes after this judgment.

A target is an existing representation that an expansion produces. Three targets exist, and they are distinct:

TargetProposals that it produces
Finite projectFinite Raw and QIRF proposals
Selected RawSelected-source Raw proposals
Selected hierarchySelected hierarchy and finite-leaf proposals

The Target field of a contract identifies an existing representation on one or more of these targets. It does not claim that one canonical typed Core or one converged emitter has replaced them. Each target supports a bounded profile of programs.

The native checker is the checker executable that lean-kernel/ builds. It decides whether a proposal is accepted. It decides this independently of the component that produced the proposal.

Rules:

  • The common checker checks every declaration, both arms of every branch and the body of a fold with zero iterations.
  • Static bindings and immutable facts are inputs to concrete elaboration. They are not accepted handles of the native checker.
  • A supported surface form may have an unsupported projection or target. A failure must not select a weaker checker.
  • Every resulting artifact, dependency and independent request must pass the production gate that applies to it.
  • Handwritten IR and generated IR have the same requirements for independent acceptance.
  • Four activities confer no acceptance: Rust normalization, source checking, source-step comparison and proposal generation.
  • Valid output alone does not prove source preservation.

Implementation note. The common checker is src/frontend/check.rs with the modules under src/frontend/check/. The Rust frontend parses, reports diagnostics and generates proposals. It issues no acceptance.

Evaluation and owner lifetime boundaries

This section states when the parts of an expression evaluate and where the lifetime of a quantum owner may end.

An access call is a call that marks at least one argument with excl or ctrl. A place is such a marked argument. It is a whole lexical owner, or a static selection of the axes of one.

Evaluation order

Statements, arguments and tuple fields evaluate in the order in which the source writes them.

Rules:

  • For every supported expression form, statements execute in source order.
  • A call evaluates its arguments completely, once, from left to right, before it enters the callee.
  • Tuple fields follow the same rule. A nested expression evaluates before the operation that encloses it.
  • An ordinary Unit result does not suppress the effects of an argument. (h(excl q), z(excl q)); finishes H before it starts Z, just as h(excl q); z(excl q); does.
  • Ignoring a product of ordinary Unit values inserts no quantum destruction.

Owner lifetime

The rules below say when a temporary owns a quantum value, and what the end of a scope does to a live owner.

Rules:

  • A temporary may own a quantum value while that value flows to an explicit consumer, a binding or a result. measure_z(h(init0())) is such a chain.
  • The end of an expression statement, a wildcard binding, a shadowed binding or a local block must not silently destroy a live quantum owner. The checker rejects an unreturned local owner at its binding. It rejects a discarded quantum expression at that expression. Both routes report the code ownership.
  • Ordinary Bit, Unit and product values may be ignored.
  • The language has no observable classical destructor and no quantum drop glue.
  • The exit from a scope cannot choose measurement, discard, reset or clean discharge for the programmer.

Places and access calls

The rules below say what a place is and when the caller can use its owners again after an access call.

Rules:

  • A place is formed by its call. It is not a reference value with an inferred lifetime.
  • An access call supplies its owners in argument order and executes the callee once.
  • The call restores the lexical operation rights only after it retains the returned values and completes any reassembly of a selected parent. This happens before the next argument, tuple field or statement starts.
  • A call inside the body of the callee completes before the enclosing call returns.
  • The end of a surrounding block preserves the updated outer owner. It does not restore an earlier state.
  • Four forms are unsupported: stored or escaping views, nested place projections, implicit method autoref and arbitrary reference lifetimes.
  • A static axis selector uses the original resolved static context and has no runtime effect.
  • A shorter lifetime grants no control capability, restores no dirty workspace and proves no clean release. The distinct obligations for effect, sector and exact workspace apply. These rules supply no workspace syntax and no evidence.

Order of operations

The rules below say what elaboration retains of the written order, and what a reordering needs.

Rules:

  • Elaboration retains the ordered source steps, the call boundaries, the original owners and the source locations. Raw instructions retain the ordered edges of their owner tokens.
  • Scheduling convenience does not permit a reorder of operations that do not commute. Neither do names that appear to be separate.
  • A proposed reordering needs a separately justified transformation that preserves exact phase, reference behavior and all applicable contracts.
  • Source evaluation follows the written order. A transformation of executable code requires its own evidence for the changed owner flow and for the other contracts.

Implementation note. Independent source replay rejects an altered gate chain even when the type, effect and ownership interface of that chain is native-valid.

A whole-owner exclusive call lets a program transform an owner and keep its name. A call such as h(excl q) supplies the live lexical owner q to one consuming call. The same binder then retains the returned quantum value.

The effect of the callee follows the public-effect rule. The effect that counts depends on the module that contains the call.

Rules:

  • Outside the original module of the callee, the checked public contract counts. Inside that module, internal inference counts.
  • A body can have a sharper effect than its public contract. Specialization, inlining and cache reuse cannot turn that sharper effect into eligibility for external source.

Ordinary Unit arguments in access calls

An access call can take ordinary arguments beside its marked places. A checked mixed call passes () or a lexical Unit value beside its marked places.

Rules:

  • The call evaluates the original arguments once, in their written order. It reserves a marked owner when it reaches that argument.
  • A later argument must not use a reserved owner as an ordinary value.
  • A quantum owner such as Q<Unit> needs an access marker and an ownership check, like every other owner.
  • The named callee receives the complete ordered value list, including each Unit at its source position.
  • The independently checked Raw interface carries only the marked quantum ports.
  • The callee returns those owner types in their original relative order. The call restores them to their original places and returns ordinary Unit.
  • The checked operation retains its exact phase and the same interface to untouched owners.
  • An exclusive named call may also take ordinary Bit arguments when the selected callee lowers to a single checked ordinary branch.

The selected-entry route with the native checker accepts update:

unitary fn turn(tag: Unit, data: Q<Bit>) -> Q<Bit> { h(data) }

pub unitary fn update(data: Q<Bit>) -> Q<Bit> {
    turn((), excl data);
    data
}

Implementation note. The native source-body check validates the named call, the returned owners, the Raw interval and any extraction and reinsertion. For an access body without selection, basis or Meaning evidence, it requires an original access receipt and complete coverage of the selected invocation; see the native scope. For ordinary Bit arguments, it uses the original condition and both Raw arms. It verifies each parked owner in both arms and at the checked branch return, and it preserves port order and zero-width owners. The dormant checker admits a mixed ctrl call from a closed nongeneric body after the native source check covers that exact original occurrence. It checks the marked quantum sectors of the callee again.

Declared access parameters

A function can write the access marker in its own signature. A finite function with a closed selected specialization may declare a marked owner slot, as in target: excl Q<Bit>, and write a Unit result.

Rules:

  • The call must supply the exact marker at that argument position.
  • The body must check as Unitary and must leave each marked owner live on its original binder.
  • The ordinary result is Unit at the written call. The body may return () or a value of type Unit, including a parameter.
  • The returned quantum owners carry the updated state, including its exact phase.
  • A declared access requires the finite check of the original body. For every ctrl slot it also requires the applicable exact sector check.

The selected-entry route with the native checker accepts update:

pub unitary fn flip(target: excl Q<Bit>) -> Unit {
    h(excl target);
    ()
}

pub unitary fn update(target: Q<Bit>) -> Q<Bit> {
    flip(excl target);
    target
}

Implementation note. The native call boundary reads the marked owners in parameter order and verifies the returned Raw ports. The production source check retains the declaration and the marked call as original occurrences. When it checks a specialization, it also retains explicit static arguments such as type(Bit).

Rules:

  • Different parent names alone are not evidence of different axes. The checks use resolved binding identities.
  • ctrl keeps its separate original sector obligation. The annotation grants no permission.
  • The common checker checks every original declaration, both branch bodies and the body of every fold.
  • The premises of static branches and of loops take part in the arithmetic proof. This includes impossible contexts, as for other static obligations.
  • The checks of names, types, effects and ownership cover unexecuted code as well.

Implementation note. The common AST represents each selection as a parent-owner occurrence and a static selector. The project compiler submits its emitted program to native Raw checking. The selected path also replays source steps against the partitions and the reassembly. A dormant action with closed ports can be checked without evaluating the register extraction of an inactive branch. On the Selected hierarchy target, source replay checks the original ordered ports of each partition and the phase-free graph interval. For supported pure ctrl calls, replay binds the original ordered call interval in that same graph. Lean checks the sectors of the exact action during the invocation that checks the executable hierarchy.

The bounded resource correspondence counts the live wires of the indexed Raw access and its reassembly. The selected operation and any emitted physical implementation have their own resource checks.

Explicit maps and unsupported candidates

Some maps are explicit operations of the source program. The program writes them, and no elaboration inserts them. This section also names conveniences that the language does not admit.

Rules:

  • split/join, Unit insertion and removal, and the register primitives are explicit specified maps. They keep their original ordered interfaces and the coefficient +1.
  • An elaboration must not insert such a map to repair an argument tree.
  • Bit, Bits<1>, Unit and Bits<0> are distinct. Tuples of different arity or nesting are distinct. Packed owners are distinct from separate owners.
  • Canonical reassociation is not definitional equality. It is not an admitted implicit elaboration.
  • An ordinary literal does not prepare a quantum state.
  • init0 is not a primitive. It is the ordinary standard-library function std::state::init0, declared pub isometry fn init0() -> Q<Bit> and defined by basis unit(()) as _ { 0 }. It keeps its explicit Isometry contract.
  • basis is a coherent map. It is not sugar for arbitrary state preparation.
  • Beyond explicit selected-place access, no implicit view partition and no implicit reassembly is admitted.
  • No insertion of excl or ctrl, no arbitrary wire permutation and no cleanup is admitted.
  • A convenience may be added only after its complete contract and its checking evidence exist. The candidate list of an Issue is insufficient.

Register primitives on the Selected Raw target

On the Selected Raw target, the register primitives take_bit[N,K] and put_bit[N,K] expand into structural instructions.

Rules:

  • Each requires its statically established premise K < N.
  • Each expands to three existing Split/Join instructions.
  • take_bit preserves the ordered remainder. put_bit inserts the bit at its declared axis.
  • Empty prefixes, empty suffixes and the Bits<0> remainder are logical owners through the structural operations.
  • The expansion emits no physical SWAP, preparation, measurement or implicit discard.

These primitives and the lowering of selected places use the same structural instructions. Explicit indexed access and slice access connect common checking to ordered partition and reconstruction under the static-place rules. Such access does not insert a source take_bit/put_bit call, and it does not infer an access mode.

Implementation note. Independent source-step replay checks the original partition, the intermediate fresh owners and the returned wire order of each take_bit and put_bit. Native validity is a separate fresh decision.

Original locations and failure

This section states the original location that each emitted operation retains, and how kinds of failure stay distinct.

Rules:

  • A span is a half-open UTF-8 byte interval in the unnormalized original source.
  • Emitted operations retain their origins in the original expression and body. Finite nested branches retain operation and arm-index paths.
  • An enclosing expression must not overwrite the origin of a child or of a callee.
  • An inserted operation without a more specific origin uses the span of its expression.
  • Unsupported profiles and work limits keep distinct diagnostics. Exhaustion is not mathematical invalidity.
  • A migration error names the original tokens.

Implementation note. After a finite native rejection, diagnostic mapping selects the longest retained operation-path prefix, and otherwise the original caller or declaration. This mapping is post-rejection metadata only. It cannot change acceptance, manufacture evidence or hide a failure.

Proofs

The items below describe the theorems that concern the forms of this chapter, and what those theorems do not establish.

  • The compatible access theorem uses the ordered physical wires of each checked call as its footprint. Any wire that both calls use must be ctrl in both. The exact local reconstruction preserves every shared control bit, and the other modified coordinates are disjoint.
  • For a checked pair of adjacent calls with a structural return, the theorem states an equality of complex amplitudes. The original Raw root and the exchanged operator composition agree after the same output permutation, for every input and every external reference column. The equality retains conditional phase and entanglement.
  • A protected-phase theorem exchanges adjacent literal phases in their original physical action.
  • Qleisli.SharedControlCommutation proves exact matrix equality for common control sectors that act on disjoint ordered target factors. Its single-control bridge concerns the hierarchy definitions of tensor, control and sequence. The bridge includes unchanged surrounding operations and arbitrary amplitudes of an external reference. Zero-width factors retain their scalar phases.
  • Qleisli.RoutedControlCommutation extends that connection to two original hierarchy entry bodies with distinct intermediate routes. Its explicit premises bind the original definition records, same-artifact node typing, fresh wiring summaries, computed two-sided inverse permutations and provider evaluations. Literal coordinate decompositions identify a common control, disjoint ordered targets and untouched factors. The resulting exact matrix equality preserves arbitrary amplitudes of an external reference.
  • Typing alone establishes neither those coordinate decompositions nor matching provider meanings. The two commutation results derive no source footprint from ctrl or &mut. They prove no arbitrary routing of owners or axes, and they authorize no source reordering. Width premises do not discharge Q<Unit> ownership. Four obligations are open: source footprint, general lowering, acceptance and work preservation.
  • Neither commutation proof is a constitutional guarantee or a guarantee admission. Their bounded examples and proof records are in tests/fixtures/constitution_v030/shared-control-commutation/ and tests/fixtures/constitution_v030/routed-control-commutation/. The emitted-source examples of the second distinguish the conditional Lean theorem from runtime checks and bounded matrix observations. Their zero-axis sources use Q<Bits<0>>, not an identification with Q<Unit>.
  • The QPTL theorem QPTL.FiniteMatrix.scratchMarginal_eq_iff proves a partial-trace equivalence for finite Kraus families on positive semidefinite unit-trace inputs. It concerns a finite trace-preserving channel whose workspace starts in a fixed pure state.
  • The equivalence has two sides. One side is the exact return of the workspace marginal on every live input. The other side is factorization with the same workspace state for every joint input with an external reference.
  • In a scratch basis that contains the clean state, the unwanted diagonal populations are sums of squared Kraus coefficients. Their vanishing on all live basis inputs forces every unwanted coefficient to vanish.
  • The live output includes any declared classical outcome register with its original probabilities. The live factor is the joint output with scratch traced out, and it retains the reference coordinates.
  • The exact requested live action, the coherent phase, the dirty-workspace identity and the quantitative costs have their own contracts.

Preservation

  • Independent source replay is not a general source-preservation theorem and not a proof of an optimizer.
  • General source preservation and quantitative resource correspondence are proof obligations.
  • The bounded migration comparisons are migration evidence. They are not universal source preservation, complete QS/PR/RS, a generic QFT implementation or release approval.

Guarantees and specification review

  • Two scoped guarantees are admitted: QS-QLV1-OWNERSHIP-2026-01 for ownership and QS-QLV1-SCOPE-2026-01 for classical scope. Both keep their original decoded-root premises.
  • Four obligations are pending: the broader source and semantic QS obligation, the forward PR obligation, the quantitative RS obligation and the EXACT obligation. Their interpretations are QS-2026-01, PR-2026-01, RS-2026-01 and EXACT-2026-01.
  • The contracts of this chapter are adopted source contracts. A contract table discharges no guarantee and adds no ledger entry.
