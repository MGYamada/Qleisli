## Problem

The Constitution constrains Qleisli only if ordinary language evolution is routinely checked against the Fundamental Theorems.

Today, an enhancement can be discussed in terms of syntax, ergonomics, implementation difficulty, or local semantics without explicitly recording whether it:

- preserves a Fundamental Theorem;
- strengthens it;
- changes one of its premises;
- adds a new normative dependency;
- changes the constitutional kernel;
- adds a new trusted assumption.

That makes constitutional reasoning optional exactly where it should become habitual.

## Proposal

Beginning with the 0.3.0 design process, every language-level feature, breaking semantic change, or constitutional-kernel change should include a short **Fundamental-Theorem impact analysis**.

A candidate template is:

```text
Constitutional impact

Qleisli Soundness:
  preserves / strengthens / affects / not applicable

Physical Realizability:
  preserves / strengthens / affects / not applicable

Resource Safety:
  preserves / strengthens / affects / not applicable

Constitutional-kernel change:
  yes / no

New normative definition:
  yes / no

New trusted assumption:
  yes / no

Ledger impact:
  existing entries / new binding interpretation / none with reason

Current acceptance/artifact binding:
  unchanged / changed with preservation evidence

Semantic transport:
  existing / new obligation / not needed with reason

Proof coverage:
  discharged by existing proof / new proof required / pending

Edition impact:
  same edition / requires edition review
```

The exact form may be implemented as an issue template, PR template, checklist, or generated metadata, but the semantic requirement should be normative.

## Scope

The requirement should apply at least to changes involving:

- public syntax with semantic consequences;
- typing/effect/ownership rules;
- static evaluation;
- `Op`, `Meaning`, traits/capabilities, and constraints;
- measurement/instrument semantics;
- realizability rules;
- resource semantics;
- the constitutional core/checker/accepted representation;
- protected normative definitions.

Pure documentation, diagnostics wording, test refactoring, and implementation-only changes outside the constitutional boundary may use a lightweight “no constitutional impact” declaration.

## Important distinction

This mechanism does **not** require every feature author to provide a new formal proof.

Its purpose is to force one explicit design question:

> **How does this change live under the three Fundamental Theorems?**

Existing proofs may already cover a change. The author records that coverage and affected ledger entries; the Guardian has final interpretation authority when a new constitutional case or dispute arises. Routine changes covered by existing interpretations do not require a new individual ruling.

Newly activated duties must be discharged before corresponding certification. Pending obligations must remain explicit under #129 Article VIII. Later interpretation cannot waive a discharged ledger guarantee, and changing a premise cannot excuse currently accepted cases from its protection.

The impact analysis must distinguish a same-edition extension or formal strengthening from constitutional amendment. An edition transition does not override Article IV's non-weakening rule.

## Core principle

> **The Constitution should become a design procedure, not merely a document.**

A new Qleisli feature should be justified not only by what it enables, but by where it sits under Soundness, Physical Realizability, and Resource Safety.
