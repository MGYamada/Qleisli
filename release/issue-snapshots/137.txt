## Status

Constitutional versioning issue for **Qleisli 0.3.0**.

Related: #129 (Constitution Draft 5), #135 (ledger and acceptance identity), #139 (jurisdictions), #141 (CI), #255 (explicit qrate edition).

## Problem

Release versions and constitutional editions are different axes. A version may change without amending the Constitution, and the language may gain expressive power within the same edition.

The previous model of one frozen `Accepted_e` and one complete semantic vocabulary per edition would incorrectly make such growth require an edition transition.

## Adopted version model

An edition identifies the entrenched constitutional framework and its continuing obligations. A release/formalization state identifies the current implementation, acceptance judgments, and semantic realization.

Conceptually:

```text
Constitution_e
Jurisdictions_e
Ledger_e,t

Accepted_e,t(P)
Meaning_e,t(P)
RealizationAccepted_e,t(P, target, artifact, evidence)
```

The indices are explanatory notation, not names or syntax mandated by the Constitution.

For example, different releases may all belong to edition 2026 while using stronger current formal realizations. The edition's constitutional text stays fixed; its ledgered guarantees accumulate.

An edition identifies the constitutional regime, not a syntax generation, grammar, source dialect, or compiler release. Syntax, type-system, frontend, CLI, and compatibility changes are governed by release versioning and the published migration policy. The unified 0.3.0 frontend remains in edition 2026; grammar changes must not implicitly select or require another edition.

Initial ratification is the first dedicated stage of the approved 0.3.0 work. The natural-human project maintainer affirmatively adopts the exact constitutional text for edition 2026, with the adopting authority, text identity, and effective date recorded. Appointment of the Constitution Guardian Office is a separate recorded act. Selecting release 0.3.0, approving a work plan, or generating a manifest does not itself constitute ratification.

## What changes require a new edition?

- Except for a valid pre-v1 rectification authorized under #129 Article IX, a change to ratified constitutional text requires a new edition and an explicit amendment.
- Addition of another Fundamental-Theorem jurisdiction requires that amendment process (#138).
- Applying an existing jurisdiction to a new case is constitutional interpretation (#149).
- Extending proofs, changing source syntax or types, adding a language feature, replacing an implementation, or recording a discharged interpretation does not by itself require a new edition. Such changes remain subject to constitutional obligations, non-weakening, and the published release compatibility and migration policy.
- A new edition does not override #129 Article IV's non-weakening rule.

Use **same-edition extension**, **constitutional interpretation**, **formal strengthening**, and **constitutional amendment** distinctly.

## Historical integrity without freezing the language

Historical releases and ledger entries retain their recorded meaning; they must not be silently redefined. The current acceptance mechanism may change, but it must discharge current jurisdiction-wide obligations and every applicable historical ledger guarantee.

A translation of an old fragment must have a justified relation to its recorded semantics and the current accepted artifacts. #135 owns this transport requirement.

No law such as

```text
Accepted_e,t ⊆ Accepted_e,t+1
```

is imposed merely by the safety Constitution. Acceptance-set continuity is a separate compatibility promise. In particular, restricting acceptance is not by itself weakening a safety implication, although it may violate release/source compatibility policy.

## Compatibility and support

Distinguish:

1. **Constitutional continuity** — preserved jurisdictions, accumulated guarantees, semantic transport, and current production proof coverage.
2. **Language/source compatibility** — whether old programs remain accepted with the promised behavior.
3. **Implementation support** — maintained parsers, toolchains, backends, and migration paths.

Preserving recorded guarantees does not imply perpetual support for every old binary or frontend. Compatibility policy and QLT/corpus regression must state the actual support promise.

An implementation may not exploit this separation to accept a historical program while evading its applicable ledgered guarantee.

## v1.0.0

v1.0.0 requires full discharge for constitutional conformance under #129 Article VIII. It does not freeze the acceptance relation or concrete theorem types.

Same-edition language growth can continue after v1.0.0. The new feature's obligations must be discharged before certification, and historical guarantees remain in force.
