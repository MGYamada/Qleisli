Qleisli Programming Language

Policy, constitutional harness and execution

Policy as Theorem. Constitution as Harness. Compiler as Executor.

This is Qleisli’s top-level architecture principle. It has three parts, and this chapter gives a section to each. The principle is subordinate to the authority hierarchy, the ratified Constitution and the recorded human interpretations. It defines no mathematical interpretation and no discharged guarantee.

The qrate and external-artifact boundary distinguishes five kinds of role: semantic, evidence, observational, realization and organizational. It states explicitly the current package limitations and edition-binding limitations.

Policy as Theorem

A policy that affects constitutional acceptance must become a formal proposition, or an evidence obligation, whose discharge can be checked independently. An implementation convention, a theorem name, a test or a successful compilation cannot supply that authority.

The Constitution Guardian, a natural human, determines the binding constitutional interpretation for a defined case. The interpretation says what must be proved, which scope and premises apply, and how the requirement relates to existing guarantees. Lean, or the designated independent proof checker, establishes that the resulting proposition is derivable under its actual definitions and recorded assumptions. Human interpretation and mathematical verification are separate judgments. Ordinary maintainers select language and implementation designs within those requirements. Routine covered maintenance needs no further interpretation.

QS, PR and quantitative RS are three permanent, distinct proof obligations. Their binding meanings come from the adopted interpretations. This chapter freezes no predicate and no theorem type. Evidence must connect the claimed property to the actual production acceptance and to the current accepted artifact. A source claim additionally requires its source-preservation link. A realization claim requires correspondence to the actual output. A resource claim requires the specified bound for the actual behavior or realization. A proof about a convenient unused predicate cannot replace those links.

The initial interpretations and scoped guarantees record current coverage. Only ordinary QLV1 root ownership and classical scope have been admitted as scoped guarantees. Broader QS, PR, quantitative RS and the EXACT supplement retain pending duties. Passing a compiler check does not discharge them. The Fundamental Theorems do not themselves prove any of these components correct: the Rust frontend, the native compiler, the decoder, the simulator or the exporter. Each claimed correspondence retains its own obligations and assumptions.

Constitution as Harness

The Constitution fixes four things: permanent obligations, human interpretive authority, recording procedures and continuity. Discharged guarantees accumulate for their recorded scopes and premises. A future syntax, provider, backend, representation, release or tool cannot escape an applicable obligation by renaming its acceptance path or by moving it. A changed representation requires the appropriate checked semantic transport. Old proof files alone are insufficient. Edition identifies this constitutional regime. Syntax compatibility and release compatibility are separate matters.

The harness must reach executable boundaries. Prefer making a prohibited operation unrepresentable, untypable, unverifiable or explicitly rejected. External prompts, coding conventions and author identity cannot be the only protection for an accepted guarantee. Identity, continuity, production coverage and required evidence belong in mechanical enforcement, with their actual limitations exposed. The CI checks implement parts of that harness. Constitutional CI and the release gate have unfinished requirements. History links to them.

Pre-v1 proof maturity permits the truthful disclosure of incomplete coverage. It never permits any of these: withdrawing an admitted guarantee, treating a failed check as successful, or advertising an unproved scope as constitutionally certified.

Compiler as Executor

The compiler executes policy; it does not create policy.

The language and its ecosystem are the executable surface of these constraints. These steps apply the authorized contracts: parsing, name resolution, the preparation of types, effects and ownership, the generation of evidence, and lowering. Native Lean checking supplies each fresh semantic accepted handle under the constitutional theorem boundary. Rust proposals, authored source and external IR are all subject to the applicable independent checks. When native checking is absent, incompatible or fails, the result is a rejection. Nothing falls back to another acceptance authority.

Crossing a protected boundary must fail closed and explain the violated rule. A safe expressible alternative may be suggested when one exists. Unsupported constructs, missing evidence and failed native checks must never silently become any of these: weaker semantics, a hidden discard or reset, implicit physical coercions, or unchecked backend acceptance. A diagnostic may explain a proof gap. It cannot waive the gap.

Diagnostics and inspectable recovery

Diagnostics are constitutional pedagogy: they make an enforced boundary visible to humans and coding agents. A useful rejection identifies the boundary and the likely classical assumption. It also identifies the canonical Qleisli construct, where one is available, and a recovery that preserves the contract. For example, when an explicit Op provider is missing, the rejection names the unresolved binding. It does not search for a convenient implementation. A quantum wildcard must not silently consume an owner. Text and JSON describe the same failure and source location. The diagnostic reference lists categories, recovery guidance, and conformance cases.

An unfamiliar derived stdlib abstraction should have an inspectable recovery path. The path is to read its signature, inspect the ordinary checked .qli implementation, and then follow its calls to language rules and specified primitives. Derived code must undergo the same applicable checking as user code. Source location, first-party status and documentation generation confer no semantic authority. Language primitives require their canonical contracts and independent checks. Reading an implementation cannot reconstruct or authorize a different meaning. The primitive inventory and current limitations separate this policy from the source cutover and from the open proof obligations and profile obligations of that cutover.

Three Qleisli tools are planned: QLT, the Qleisli Testsuite; QDB, the Qleisli Debugger; and QCP, the Qleisli Circuit Profiler. They and qargo, qlidoc and other tools are subordinate interfaces. They may expose programs, facts, diagnostics or evidence. They cannot adopt interpretations, waive proof, create a parallel acceptance path or weaken accepted meaning. Their implementation is later work. This chapter installs none of those tools. mdBook is the separate build environment for the documentation. It creates no language authority and no API-introspection authority.

Inference and explicit discovery

This section sorts the concepts of the language and its tools into two categories. The categories say how an author who meets a concept for the first time finds its meaning.

For human authors and coding agents, zero-prior inferable means that an author can recover the intended semantic role without knowing Qleisli-specific names. Familiar spelling or similar-looking code alone is insufficient. Explicitly discoverable means that a canonical introduction exposes the role and its contract. The author does not have to guess the name of the construct first. These are categories for documentation and design. They are not claims about the measured success rate of an LLM, and they are not permission to infer quantum meaning.

ConceptCategory and recovery path
Ordinary modules, imports, tuples and bindersInferable where their ordinary meaning survives. The source/type Reference and located diagnostics identify the exact syntax and predictable classical mistakes.
Quantum owners, effects, access capabilities and MeaningExplicit discovery through the type, Rust and functional boundary chapters. Classical familiarity cannot supply their quantum obligations.
Derived stdlib abstractionsExplicit discovery through the signature and mathematical contract, then ordinary .qli source under stdlib/src/ and its callees. Source inspection does not itself prove the contract.
Language primitives and external providersExplicit discovery through the primitive and production boundaries. Reading derived source cannot reconstruct or grant their semantic authority.
First-party ecosystem tools, including QLT, QDB and QCPExplicit discovery through qleisli --help, then qleisli help ecosystem. Each tool’s role and current availability are stated there. These are Qleisli-native discovery targets.

The help path works without a project and without a native checker. It issues no accepted handle. Planned tools are unavailable for execution. Predictable classical mistakes need correction paths that are deterministic and located and that preserve owners, phase and evidence. Semantic ambiguity must reject. Naming must avoid misleading pseudo-analogues and semantic false friends. Every future first-party addition to the language or to the ecosystem must declare its category, its intended semantic role and its canonical recovery or discovery path. This implements the documentation boundary for inference and discovery within the Reference.

Required review of future designs

This section is a checklist for the author of a design document. Every major design document for the language or for its tooling must identify how it preserves:

  1. the applicable adopted interpretation, scope, premises and existing guarantees;
  2. formal obligations, actual acceptance/artifact bindings, checked evidence and open proof gaps;
  3. authorized source rules, lowering, ownership/effects, exact phase/axes and fail-closed behavior;
  4. diagnostic recovery, inspectable derived source and explicit tool discovery;
  5. the distinction between human interpretation, formal verification and execution.

Use the existing constitutional impact procedure and the GitHub decision record. An unresolved constitutional case that existing interpretations do not cover requires the concrete human judgment. Ordinary implementation within existing interpretations is maintenance. No design document gains constitutional authority by completing this checklist.
