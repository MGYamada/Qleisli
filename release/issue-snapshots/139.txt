## Problem

The Constitution identifies three Fundamental Theorems:

1. **Qleisli Soundness Theorem**
2. **Physical Realizability Theorem**
3. **Resource Safety Theorem**

The wrong model is to treat these as three Lean theorem types that must eventually be frozen word-for-word.

That would make future same-edition language growth constitutionally brittle. A new feature could create a genuine new proof obligation that the old theorem syntax did not quantify over, forcing either an artificial edition change or an illicit escape from the theorem.

The Constitution is intended to prevent exactly that.

## Adopted model: theorem jurisdiction, not frozen theorem syntax

Each Fundamental Theorem names an **enduring constitutional proof jurisdiction** of edition 2026.

The Constitution fixes:

- the existence of the three jurisdictions;
- their protected purpose;
- the rule that every admitted edition-2026 feature remains subject to them;
- the prohibition on weakening or evading them.

It does **not** freeze one forever-complete Lean proposition that must anticipate every future construct.

Conceptually:

```text
Constitution 2026
    ├── Soundness jurisdiction
    ├── Physical Realizability jurisdiction
    └── Resource Safety jurisdiction

current language L_t
    ↓
current formal realization of each jurisdiction
    ↓
Lean theorem(s) / checked evidence for L_t
```

When the language grows from `L_t` to `L_{t+1}`, the concrete theorem statements may grow as well while remaining realizations of the same constitutional obligations.

## Constitution Guardian Office interpretation authority

The **Constitution Guardian Office has final and exclusive authority to interpret how the three Fundamental-Theorem jurisdictions apply to new same-edition features and constitutional disputes**.

For a proposed feature `F`, the Guardian determines:

- which Fundamental-Theorem jurisdictions apply;
- what new constitutional proof obligation is activated;
- whether the existing formal realization already covers `F`;
- whether a stronger theorem or additional evidence is required;
- whether the change remains constitutionally continuous within edition 2026.

The Guardian does not establish mathematical truth by interpretation.

The authority split is:

```text
Constitution
    fixes enduring jurisdiction

Constitution Guardian Office
    interprets that jurisdiction for the new case

Lean / independent checker
    proves or rejects the resulting obligation

ordinary maintainers
    choose implementation inside the interpreted boundary
```

A Constitution Guardian Office interpretation cannot waive a failed proof or redefine a false proposition as true.

## Current formal realizations

0.3.0 should still draft concrete theorem schemas because the implementation needs actual objects to prove today.

For example:

```text
Soundness_t:
  Accepted_t(P)
    -> current ownership/effect/semantic promises hold

PhysicalRealizability_t:
  Accepted_t(P)
  ∧ CheckedRealization_t(P, τ, C, E)
    -> C realizes Meaning_t(P) under the checked target/workspace contract

ResourceSafety_t:
  Accepted_t(P)
    -> current admitted resource obligations are finitely/statically accountable
```

But these are **current formal realizations**, not constitutional articles.

If edition 2026 later admits parametric families, the current RS realization may legitimately strengthen to something such as:

```text
AcceptedFamily_{t+1}(P<n>)
  -> Resource(P<n>) <= f(n)
```

without an edition change, because the Resource Safety jurisdiction already existed.

## Required 0.3.0 decisions

For each jurisdiction, document:

- its protected constitutional purpose;
- the current language objects to which it applies;
- the current formal theorem/evidence realization;
- the checker/Lean boundary that discharges it;
- which definitions are implementation-era formalization details;
- which changes merely strengthen/adapt the current proof;
- which changes require constitutional amendment and a new edition, and which would violate non-weakening even with an edition change.

Do not require the current schema to anticipate every future Qleisli construct.

## Monotonicity rule

Same-edition language growth may increase the proof burden.

It may not escape it.

```text
new feature
    ↓
Constitution Guardian Office interpretation
    ↓
existing constitutional jurisdiction(s) activate
    ↓
formal theorem/evidence strengthened as necessary
    ↓
proof required before acceptance
```

A stronger current theorem is ordinary same-edition development when it merely discharges an already-existing jurisdiction.

## Accumulated guarantees and current acceptance

The formal realizations above govern the current accepted language. They must also preserve the discharged interpretation ledger from #135.

A binding Constitution Guardian Office interpretation, once formally discharged and recorded, becomes a minimum guarantee. Later interpretations may clarify or strengthen it but may not repeal or relax it within the edition. This protection concerns the recorded property, domain, premises, and semantic meaning, not one Lean declaration's spelling.

Every release must relate its actual production acceptance and accepted artifacts to both:

- the three jurisdictions over the whole current admitted scope; and
- every applicable historical ledger obligation, with justified semantic transport.

Changing theorem premises is permitted for new cases only when it does not exempt currently accepted cases from a previously protected obligation. A proof over a narrower ghost predicate does not establish production conformance.

The v1.0.0 milestone is full discharge of these duties, not a freeze of `Accepted_t` or the top-level theorem types. Earlier proof-maturity and certification claims follow #129 Article VIII and #142.

Forward PR is the chosen admission-scoped contract under #281. General synthesis completeness remains optional target/profile mathematics; this is a scope decision, not a claim that implication syntax alone logically forbids an existence theorem.

## Non-goals

This issue does **not**:

- freeze final Lean theorem types for the lifetime of edition 2026;
- predict every future type, control form, target profile, or resource model;
- make theorem syntax part of `CONSTITUTION.md`;
- let the Guardian waive proof obligations or alter mathematical truth;
- let ordinary maintainers redefine constitutional jurisdiction without Constitution Guardian Office interpretation.

## Core principle

> **The Constitution freezes theorem jurisdiction, not theorem syntax.**

> **The Guardian interprets the jurisdiction; Lean proves the resulting obligation.**

## Draft 5 institutional synchronization

Under #129 Draft 5, the **Constitution Guardian Office** is the permanent constitutional institution. Constitutional authority is exercised only by its natural-human holder or human members. Artificial intelligence, autonomous agents, software systems, or analogous non-human computational entities may assist with research, drafting, criticism, formalization, search, or analysis, but may never hold, receive, exercise, vote on, or proxy the Office's constitutional authority.

This institutional rule does not change the theorem jurisdictions in this issue; it fixes who may issue the binding interpretation that activates them.
