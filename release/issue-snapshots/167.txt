## Status

**Adopted 0.3.0 cutover (2026-10-12; coordinated with #369):** The *public* `std::quantum` namespace is transitional and **must be retired before final 0.3.0**. Irreducible primitives become directly available as **language-owned, unqualified intrinsic operations**, not as `std::` library imports. Derived quantum gates live exclusively as **ordinary checked `.qli`** under `std::gate`; `std::gate` is never a primitive facade. No persistent `std::quantum` compatibility aliases remain in 0.3.0. This concretizes and supersedes earlier unresolved surface-spelling options in this issue; it does not assert the migration is implemented or change edition-2026 constitutional authority.

**Primary Qleisli 0.3.0 important. Priority: highest. Constitutional / semantic boundary.**

This issue fixes the most important architectural boundary in Qleisli 0.3.0:

> **What is a language primitive, and what must remain an ordinary derived Qleisli program?**

The adopted direction is:

> **Everything called the standard library should, in principle, be ordinary checked `.qli` source. Anything that cannot be expressed that way is not stdlib; it belongs to the constitutional language/kernel primitive boundary.**

This issue supersedes the weaker framing of “freeze stdlib contracts” alone. The stdlib semantic freeze is now a consequence of first fixing the primitive/derived boundary.

## Core architectural rule

Qleisli 0.3.0 should establish this layering:

```text
Constitutional language / kernel primitives
        ↓
ordinary checked Qleisli source
        ↓
stdlib (.qli)
        ↓
user qrates (.qli)
```

The semantic distinction is not “stdlib vs user code.”

It is:

```text
primitive
    = trusted semantic authority admitted at the language/kernel boundary

derived
    = ordinary Qleisli source checked through the normal language/verifier path
```

The standard library belongs entirely on the **derived** side.

## 1. Define the primitive boundary

A primitive is any operation or semantic constructor whose correctness/meaning is assumed or directly owned by the language/kernel rather than derived by checking ordinary Qleisli source.

The primitive inventory may include only operations genuinely required at the semantic boundary, for example categories such as:

- irreducible quantum state/unitary constructors;
- irreducible observation/instrument transitions;
- irreducible ownership/effect structure operations;
- Core forms that cannot themselves be expressed as ordinary checked Qleisli.

The exact inventory must be audited and minimized.

A primitive is **not** justified merely because:

- users write it frequently;
- it is convenient;
- a backend has a native instruction for it;
- it is hard to optimize;
- it is mathematically important;
- it historically lived under `std::quantum` or `std::observe`.

## 2. Stdlib is ordinary checked `.qli`

Adopt the project rule:

> **If something is called stdlib, its semantic implementation is ordinary checked Qleisli source.**

Therefore:

- stdlib definitions receive the same ownership/type/effect/Meaning/verifier path as user code;
- stdlib placement gives no semantic privilege;
- stdlib code cannot manufacture trusted evidence;
- stdlib code cannot introduce new primitive authority;
- stdlib correctness is inherited from Qleisli's ordinary acceptance machinery.

This is the intended long-term meaning of “standard library.”

## 3. Embed primitives in the language; remove `std::quantum` by final 0.3.0

**Final 0.3.0 source surface (decision coordinated with #369):**

- **Primitive**: an admitted, irreducible language/kernel operation, exposed directly by the language as an unqualified intrinsic spelling. Compiler recognition is not an exemption from independent meaning, native acceptance or QS/PR/RS obligations. The Reference and typed catalog must give its exact signature and semantics. A primitive is not an ordinary `std::` import.
- **`std::gate`**: only ordinary checked `.qli` **gate** definitions built from the language primitives. Gate names do not receive special sealed admission or hidden semantic authority. `std::gate::t` is the initial migration exemplar: coherent control of a primitive `global_phase_eighth` scalar on `Q<Unit>` yields exactly `diag(1, exp(iπ/4))`.
- **Other derived stdlib modules**: e.g. `std::transform`, `std::reflection`, `std::measurement` retain their own mathematical admission rules. `std::gate` is not a catch-all for everything quantum.
- **Structural helpers are not automatically gates**: the present provisional `std::gate::permute_axes` is an axis-order/representation map, not physical SWAP. Reclassify it to an appropriate *ordinary checked* semantic location or make it private before the 0.3.0 surface is frozen; do not promote it to a primitive merely to preserve the old path.

For every member of the present `std::quantum`, classify its necessity and migrate it either to a justified language primitive or to an ordinary checked `.qli` definition in the appropriate derived module. Specifically, rename the whole-system scalar `phase_eighth` to language primitive `global_phase_eighth` (not a synonym for T), derive `std::gate::t` in `.qli`, and audit aliases/compositions such as `s`, `sdg`, `tdg` and `id`; preserve full former polymorphic contracts when migrating, or explicitly record missing support. Exact primitive inventory and surface names must be frozen in the normative Language Reference.

**No public `std::quantum` name, re-export, prelude shim or hidden compatibility alias should survive the final 0.3.0 cutover.** Update active source, imports, diagnostics, tests, inventories and docs together; preserve frozen historical evidence. Rejections of legacy imports should give a concrete migration path.

The same primitive/derived truth must hold for other compiler-owned catalogues, including `std::observe`, `std::registers` and `std::classical`: audit them separately and resolve any falsely stdlib-labelled primitive by the language/core boundary. This decision specifically commits to the retirement of **`std::quantum`** now; it does not silently choose all other replacement names.

## 4. Derived forms should dominate

The T construction and public `std::quantum` retirement are a **0.3.0** requirement, not deferred to the 0.4.0 stdlib refactor (#152). A backend is free to recognize `std::gate::t` as a physical T instruction only after checked exact realization and resource accounting; this never makes its source API a language primitive.

Adopt a strong presumption:

> **If an operation can be represented faithfully as ordinary checked Qleisli source, it must remain derived.**

Examples that should normally be derived include:

- QFT;
- arithmetic routines;
- Grover reflections;
- QPE subroutines;
- derived measurements such as X-basis or parity measurements;
- generic controlled/power constructions where supported by the language;
- algorithmic subroutines;
- reusable decompositions;
- target-specific compound instructions exposed semantically through an existing contract.

Optimization may recognize or lower a derived routine specially without promoting it into the primitive boundary.

## 5. Audit the current primitive inventory

Before 0.3.0 ships, audit every current “sealed” or special operation.

For each candidate primitive answer:

1. Can it be written as ordinary checked `.qli`?
2. If not, what exact semantic capability is missing?
3. Why must that capability live at the language/kernel boundary?
4. What exact operator/instrument/ownership/effect meaning is primitive?
5. What is its exact phase/order/reference behavior?
6. How does it enter Soundness?
7. How is it physically realizable?
8. How is it accounted under Resource Safety?
9. Could a smaller primitive basis express it instead?
10. Is it really semantic, or merely an optimizer/backend intrinsic?

Any operation failing this audit should be demoted to derived source.

## 6. Freeze the derived stdlib contracts

Once the primitive boundary is fixed, freeze the semantic contracts of the public stdlib definitions that remain.

For every public stdlib item at the 0.3.0 boundary, record as applicable:

- declaration/effect class;
- input/output basis and owner shape;
- exact operator / permutation / instrument meaning;
- exact phase convention;
- logical-axis / bit-order convention;
- reference-system behavior;
- coherent-control behavior;
- cleanup obligations;
- exact vs approximate status;
- static parameters and semantic constraints;
- declared `Meaning` identity.

These contracts must be independent of file/module path.

## 7. Keep the theorem boundary small

This issue is central to all three Fundamental Theorems.

The intended theorem architecture is:

```text
small primitive kernel
      ↓
ordinary accepted Qleisli program
      ↓
Soundness / Physical Realizability / Resource Safety
      ↓
stdlib and user code uniformly
```

The stdlib should not require a second trusted semantic layer.

A desirable consequence is:

> **The stdlib is trustworthy for exactly the same reason user Qleisli programs are trustworthy: it is accepted ordinary Qleisli.**

## 8. Coordinate with primitive admission policy

#154 should be interpreted as the **future growth rule** for the primitive set fixed here.

This flagship decides the initial boundary.

#154 decides how difficult it is to expand that boundary later.

The default must remain:

```text
can be .qli
    -> derived

cannot be faithfully .qli
    -> primitive candidate
    -> constitutional admission review
```

## 9. Coordinate with 0.4.0 stdlib structure

#152 must operate only after this issue is settled.

0.4.0 may reorganize:

- namespaces;
- modules;
- file layout;
- public reexports;
- discoverability;
- qlidoc/tool metadata;
- family structure.

It must not blur the primitive/derived boundary or introduce hidden primitive authority through naming.

## 10. Prelude consequence

#58 must also follow this boundary.

A future prelude may expose convenient names, but prelude membership must not imply stdlib or primitive status.

Primitive invocations **are** surfaced as unqualified language-owned operations in final 0.3.0, with explicit language name-resolution rules; this is not an implicit `std::prelude` import of trusted operations. `std::gate::t` remains an ordinary library import.

Derived stdlib names may enter a prelude only as ordinary reexports/imports of checked `.qli` definitions.

## Non-goals

This issue does not yet require:

- new primitive kinds or unrelated future syntax beyond the inventoried 0.3.0 language-owned primitives;
- final 0.4.0 stdlib module hierarchy;
- large stdlib expansion;
- qrate registry design;
- completion of all three theorem proofs;
- proving every generic stdlib family in 0.3.0.

It does require the semantic category boundary to be fixed.

## Release identity

The most important statement of Qleisli 0.3.0 should become:

> **Qleisli 0.3.0 fixes the line between what the language must trust and what the language can prove/check as ordinary Qleisli.**

Equivalently:

```text
primitive = constitutional kernel
derived   = Qleisli itself
stdlib    = derived Qleisli
```

This boundary should be treated as more fundamental than any particular surface syntax or module layout.

## Design intent

The goal is not merely to shrink a builtin list.

It is to make Qleisli's architecture structurally honest:

> **Anything that needs trust is language. Anything that does not need trust should be written in Qleisli.**

That is the boundary from which the three Fundamental Theorems, the standard library, tooling, optimization and the future package ecosystem should all follow.
