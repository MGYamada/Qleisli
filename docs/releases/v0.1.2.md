## Purpose and compatibility

0.1.2 is a documentation and design release containing the initial
imaginary Qleisli 1.0 algorithm corpus required by the
[pre-0.2.0 prerequisite](../release-milestones.md#pre-v020-imaginary-v1-code),
its semantic requirements, and a review of the next implementation decisions.
**All six initial algorithm drafts, their requirement records, the corpus index,
and semantic review are complete as design artifacts.** The later
[English consolidation](../documentation-map.md) translates ten current design,
planning, overview, and paper-proof documents while retaining their status.
The algorithm drafts remain imaginary
and uncompiled. The [English language framework](../language-evolution.md) was
prepared first as P012-0, before starting the corpus goal.

The patch preserves current public syntax, standard APIs, Rust/IR/evidence
APIs, Lean declarations, CLI behavior, ownership/effect rules, exact phase and
layout conventions, capacity limits, and supported toolchains. Dependencies
remain unchanged and no migration is planned. Imaginary syntax and APIs are
explicitly design proposals, not additions to the current grammar or executable
standard library. Implementing new public functionality requires a minor
release under the versioning policy.

The existing finite contract path `U E_in = E_out u` and its independently
checked final-IR evidence remain the foundation. Its finite acceptance results
do not establish general Rust implementation adequacy or complete the open
[Stage 1 proof obligations](../formal-core.md). Maintenance, regressions, and
that proof work can continue alongside the corpus.

## Design artifacts

The [corpus index](../imaginary-v1/README.md),
[shared requirements](../imaginary-v1/requirements.md), and
[semantic review](../imaginary-v1/review.md) are recorded in a separate
documentation directory. Each draft must display an algorithm body and
its composition; names or calls to undefined whole-algorithm functions are
insufficient. The bodies may use imaginary syntax and need not compile.

| Draft artifact | Structure to make visible | Important contract questions to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [QPE](../imaginary-v1/qpe.md) | Prepare the phase register, compose controlled powers, apply inverse QFT, measure, and decode. | Phase-fixed controlled/power access, precision and bit order, eigenstate promises versus general inputs, outcome/residual-state instrument. |
| [Grover](../imaginary-v1/grover.md) | Prepare the search state, compose the marked-state oracle and preparation-state reflection, apply an iteration policy, measure, and validate a candidate. | Preparation/inverse access, exact reflection signs, marked fraction or success assumptions, iteration policy and failed candidates. |
| [Amplitude estimation](../imaginary-v1/amplitude-estimation.md) | Construct the amplification operation from preparation and the good-subspace predicate, reuse QPE for a phase-based initial draft, and convert estimates to amplitudes or probabilities. | Exact amplification phase convention, controlled access, phase ambiguity, estimation error and success probability, classical interpretation. |
| [Shor](../imaginary-v1/shor.md) | Perform classical preprocessing and base selection, construct modular multiplication, reuse QPE, reconstruct and validate a period candidate, extract factors, and retry when needed. | Coprimality, reversible arithmetic on the entire register space, precision, actual sampling, period/factor validation, host boundary and retry budget. |
| [Quantum walk](../imaginary-v1/quantum-walk.md) | Choose and name a walk model, prepare its state, construct its step from visible components, iterate or use a spectral subroutine, and interpret observations. | Vertex/edge or coin spaces, unitary step and exact phases, graph/oracle access, invariant subspaces, promised task and observation semantics. |
| [QSVT](../imaginary-v1/qsvt.md) | State the block-encoding interface, obtain a phase sequence, compose the alternating signal transformations, and expose how the resulting block is used or observed. | Normalization and projected subspaces, polynomial/phase-sequence conditions, inverse/access requirements, approximation error, success branch and auxiliary treatment. |

The six drafts test shared abstractions; they do not expand the executable v1
acceptance target beyond Shor, QPE, and Grover. The selected walk model and QSVT
convention must be explicit rather than silently combining incompatible
variants. Other amplitude-estimation approaches can motivate later revisions.

Each draft and its index must record:

- Intended inputs, outputs, parameters, mathematical meaning, assumptions,
  and links between algorithm stages and reusable components.
- Required access capabilities, ownership transfer, effects, exact phase and
  axis/bit conventions, and auxiliary zero-return obligations, including
  behavior with an arbitrary reference system.
- Accuracy, statistical success/failure, classical processing and retry
  assumptions, plus circuit-generation, oracle, execution, and classical costs
  at the level justified by the draft.
- Missing syntax, APIs, evidence rules, and source-to-IR support. Classify
  each proposed facility as a **language form**, **sealed built-in operation**,
  **ordinary definition**, or **unresolved**. Record intended types,
  ownership/effects, acceptance/rejection examples, and the proposed IR route
  where known; give totality and types for basis functions.
- Open questions and candidate counterexamples, with separate draft,
  specification, implementation, validation, and proof statuses.

Unresolved contracts are allowed in the initial corpus when they are explicit
and traceable. This does not authorize their implementation or make a draft
an executable example. Approximate algorithm accuracy must remain distinct
from exact pure auxiliary cleanup. In particular, a projected block or a
successful measurement branch is not an unconditional pure operation, and
small leakage is not zero-return evidence.

## Work after the corpus review

1. Select the smallest v0.2.0 generalization scope justified by the reviewed
   drafts. Specify size and operation parameters, required access capabilities,
   and any instrument/accuracy contracts in English, with accepted/rejected
   examples, compatibility effects, capacity bounds, and source-to-IR rules.
2. Implement that selected scope after the prerequisite is recorded. Preserve
   the finite meaning contracts through composition, implementation substitution,
   and final-IR evidence; continue independent resource/effect verification.
3. Validate reusable definitions across multiple supported sizes, precisions,
   predicates or operations, as appropriate. Include phase-sensitive and
   reference-sensitive cases, failure paths, independently derived results,
   and the existing finite regressions before the v0.2.0 release checks.
4. Progress toward the unchanged
   [V1-C1–C5 conditions](../release-milestones.md#v1-acceptance-target-textbook-algorithm-structure):
   executable textbook-structured Shor, QPE, and Grover with reusable checked
   components, complete classical validation/retry behavior, and reported costs.

The exact v0.2.0 feature set is conditional on the corpus review. Neither the
0.1.2 corpus nor a later 0.2.0 release automatically completes v1, the general
compiler soundness proof, or guarantees about noisy hardware.
