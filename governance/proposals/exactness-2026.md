## Proposed ruling: scope and constitutional grounds

**Edition:** 2026. **Jurisdictions:** QS, PR and quantitative RS. This is one
interpretation applying the existing three obligations, not a fourth
Fundamental Theorem or a new discharged guarantee.

Under Articles V and VI of the [Constitution](../../CONSTITUTION.md), this
supplement applies to constitutional semantic identity and to the obligations
claimed for accepted programs, admitted realizations and resource evidence.
It clarifies and strengthens the exactness boundary of **QS-2026-01**,
**PR-2026-01** and **RS-2026-01**, subject to Article IV's non-weakening rule and
Article VII's actual-production boundary. It changes no constitutional text.

## 1. Exact truth conditions

For edition 2026, **epsilon approximation is not a constitutional semantic
primitive and cannot weaken an applicable QS, PR or RS obligation**.
An accepted semantic object has its specified exact mathematical meaning.
Floating-point comparison, a tolerance or an error budget cannot define its
semantic identity or replace a required exact proposition.

In particular, proving `distance(actual, required) <= epsilon` does not
discharge a required exact correspondence between those objects, even when
that inequality itself has an exact, mechanically checked proof. Renaming a
tolerance, placing it in a profile or choosing a convenient quotient cannot
evade this distinction. This ruling chooses no universal metric, coefficient
carrier, gate set or hardware noise model.

For **QS**, typing, ownership, effects, complete interface shape, phase,
reference behavior and workspace obligations retain their independently
specified truth conditions. A small probability of an ownership violation or
dirty ancilla is not a discharge. Exact clean return and the separate dirty
restoration contract remain those in QS-2026-01, including their all-input and
external-reference premises; no epsilon version authorizes exact release.

For **PR**, a constitutionally admitted realization must satisfy the exact
correspondence required by its accepted meaning and declared realization
contract. Schematically:

```text
Accepted(P) and AdmittedRealization_rho(P, C, E)
    imply ExactCorrespondence_rho(C, Meaning(P), E).
```

The independently specified relation must account for the actual artifact,
input/output encoding and axis order, effects, phase, applicable reference
domain, workspace and target assumptions. It cannot be replaced by closeness
to the promised meaning. This remains a forward correspondence obligation;
it creates no synthesis-completeness requirement or promise that every exact
meaning has a realization in every gate/coefficient domain.

For **RS**, quantitative bounds remain independently checked propositions
about actual accepted behavior or an actual admitted realization under the
declared resource semantics. An approximation budget cannot excuse exceeding
a required resource bound, supply missing correspondence to the source, or
turn an estimate about a different implementation into a certificate.

## 2. Phase, quotient contracts and numerical methods

Exact equality in an explicitly specified quotient is mathematically
different from epsilon approximation. This supplement does not silently
remove PR-2026-01's requirement that any proposed projective contract be
explicitly permitted and proved sound in its intended contexts. It also
admits no such contract: neither initial adoption nor this supplement grants
projective access. Equality up to phase cannot discharge an existing exact
operator or coherently controlled contract. A quotient cannot be introduced
as a tolerance mechanism or used to erase those recorded phase obligations.

Numerical computation, interval methods, approximate search and floating-point
execution are not prohibited. They may assist evidence construction when the
designated independent checker establishes the final required exact
proposition about the bound inputs and artifacts. Numerical observations or
an epsilon certificate alone do not establish that proposition. Existing
runtime approximation and floating-point assumptions must remain disclosed;
this permission proves no runtime or source-preservation theorem and creates
no new trusted primitive, project axiom or checker fallback.

The role of a parameter matters, not its spelling. If an algorithm happens to
use a parameter called epsilon but produces an independently checked exact
admitted realization, a checked bound on that actual artifact's resource cost
is not invalidated by the parameter's presence. Conversely, an exact cost
bound on a circuit that only approximates `Meaning(P)` does not supply P's
missing exact realization or certify that approximate realization as P's
constitutional target-RS evidence.

## 3. Approximate artifacts and existing implementation obligations

[Issue #31](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/31) retains its exact
source/meaning boundary and its required separate approximation contracts.
Its wording about PR connecting meanings to certified approximate target
programs must distinguish ordinary backend correspondence evidence from
edition-2026 constitutional PR: the epsilon-bound correspondence is not the
latter. Metrics, bounds, composition, reference-safe instrument treatment,
phase/control conditions and separate error categories remain required work
for approximation support; this ruling does not implement or waive them.

[Issue #163](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/163) retains its closed
precision, actual-output binding, composition, invalidation and failure rules.
When the certificate concerns an epsilon-approximate implementation of the
source meaning, it is an engineering target/resource certificate, not by
itself edition-2026 constitutional RS evidence for that source realization.
Search cost and emitted-circuit cost remain distinct. The exact-artifact
case in section 2 remains eligible for the ordinary constitutional process.

An approximate export may therefore carry useful checked engineering claims
without being advertised as an exact constitutional realization of P. If its
circuit is independently admitted as a program with its own exact meaning,
all obligations applicable to that separate accepted program still apply;
that admission does not identify its meaning with P's.

This supplement orders no reduction of the accepted source language and
grants no exemption by relabeling an existing accepted capability as external
tooling. Required source compatibility, actual acceptance and preservation
checks remain in force. Before v1, incomplete coverage must be reported under
Article VIII, rather than advertised as completed constitutional conformance.

## 4. Realization domains and future editions

Failure of exact representability in a gate/coefficient domain must be
handled by an explicitly admitted richer exact domain or scalar extension,
an applicable different realization profile, explicit unsupported realization,
or a separately labeled approximate artifact. It cannot be repaired by an
epsilon relaxation of constitutional truth conditions. No such domain or
profile extension is admitted merely by this ruling.

[Issue #309](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/309), targeted at
**0.3.1**, proposes the exact Clifford-cyclotomic coefficient tower.
[Issue #310](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/310), targeted at
**v1+**, proposes gate-set paradigms independent of constitutional edition.
They are future architectural directions, not prerequisites implemented or
pulled into 0.3.0 by this supplement. A gate/coefficient choice, hardware
target or NISQ/FTQC regime does not itself change the edition's obligations.

Introducing epsilon into constitutional semantic truth conditions is outside
this edition-2026 ruling and requires an explicit, separately reviewed future
constitutional edition transition. Before that decision, a future proposal
must specify its metric, phase convention, composition rules,
channel/instrument and reference semantics, cleanup/control/resource
interactions, acceptance identity and theorem-jurisdiction impact.
Such a transition is necessary, not sufficient: Article IV continues to
protect historical guarantees over their recorded domains, premises and
meanings. It cannot retrospectively turn an
exact phase, cleanup, correspondence or resource guarantee into a tolerance
guarantee while claiming constitutional continuity. This supplement does not
amend or bypass Articles III–VI or the Office's prescribed human procedures.
