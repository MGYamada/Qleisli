Qleisli Programming Language

Realization, specialization and resources

Checking an artifact and showing that a target implements its accepted meaning are separate tasks. A check does not settle how a target implements that meaning, how close an implementation comes, or what it costs. This chapter covers the claims that answer those three questions.

A realization is a claim that one emitted target artifact implements the accepted meaning of a program under a named target profile. A realization names a closed specialization. That is an instance of a definition in which every choice that determines its meaning and interface is fixed.

An engineering claim is an approximation claim. It states a finite bound, in a named metric, on the distance between an actual artifact and an exact ideal. It is neither acceptance nor a guarantee. A resource certificate is a checked quantitative bound on the resources of one emitted artifact.

This chapter specifies the boundary between the meaning of a program and a claimed target implementation. It applies the adopted PR-2026-01 and RS-2026-01 interpretations under the authority hierarchy.

The chapter fixes the request formats and the named profiles of the native engineering comparisons. Each of those profiles is closed and bounded, and it serves one comparison. None is the target capability schema. The chapter defines no such schema, no universal gate set and no synthesis algorithm, and it adds no source syntax. The target capability schema is future work that consumes the abstract obligations stated here.

Status and limits states the limits of the implementation and of the proofs. This chapter does not discharge the implementation and evidence obligations of the decisions that govern it.

Semantic validity and a target realization

Two questions are separate. One asks whether a program denotes a unitary operator. The other asks whether a particular target artifact implements that operator. This section keeps an answer to one from standing in for the other.

Semantic unitarity is a property of the exact denotation of a program. Physical Realizability is a forward correspondence obligation. Take an accepted closed instance p, the actual output artifact C and a profile T. A checked realization of C under T must implement the accepted meaning of p. It does so subject to its explicit premises about the interface, the encoding and the workspace.

Rules:

  • Semantic unitarity makes no promise about a target. It does not promise that the selected target can implement the operator, that synthesis can find an implementation, or that the logical wires of the source suffice.
  • When target synthesis fails, that failure must not reclassify a semantically unitary program as non-unitary.
  • Inverse access and controlled access have their own contracts. Neither follows from finding one realization.
  • A claim about a circuit that a search considered does not cover a different circuit that is emitted or executed afterwards.
  • Synthesis and search produce candidates, and they do so outside acceptance. An independently supplied candidate may qualify through the same checks. A complete synthesis algorithm for the entire semantic domain is not required.
  • A failure to find a candidate is not evidence of impossibility. An impossibility claim requires an obstruction whose hypotheses match the target.
  • A result on existence or on synthesis completeness for a whole domain is optional mathematics of a profile.

For example, Theorem 1 of Giles and Selinger establishes exact Clifford+T synthesis with at most one clean ancilla for 2^n × 2^n unitary matrices over Z[1/√2, i], under their specified gate and phase conventions. That result grants no arbitrary dirty workspace, and it does not verify the output of a particular compiler. See the original theorem.

Observing instruments on a target

This section states what a target artifact owes to a program that measures. The source instrument judgment defines the complete outcome maps of an Observe expression. Realization requires a separate judgment. That judgment is about the closed instance, the selected target profile and the actual emitted artifact.

For each ordinary input x and declared outcome c, let Φ[x,c] be the source map. Let Ψ[x,c] be the map of the emitted instrument after the declared projection of its records. The declared encoding channels are E_in for the input and E_out for the output. The exact obligation is

(Ψ[x,c] ⊗ id_R)((E_in ⊗ id_R)(ρ)) =
(E_out ⊗ id_R)((Φ[x,c] ⊗ id_R)(ρ)).

The equation holds for every finite external reference R and every joint input ρ at the complete source interface.

Rules:

  • The judgment fixes three things: the ordered quantum and classical interfaces, the logical encodings, and the projection of target records to source outcomes. It accounts for how every additional physical owner is initialized and how it is disposed of.
  • The projection assigns every target history to exactly one source outcome. This includes a history with records hidden from the source.
  • The unnormalized maps fix both the outcome probabilities and the residual quantum state on the declared retained owners.
  • The correspondence includes the consumed owners, the order of every owner that stays live and the action of every branch that contributes to a public outcome.
  • The evidence states how the profile realizes each required measurement basis, where measurements can occur, which owners stay live, and how reset or repreparation obtains its stated output.
  • For feed-forward, the evidence identifies the available classical outcome and the conditional operations. It also identifies any assumption about dynamic control, scheduling or latency that the branch needs in order to execute with the specified live owners.
  • Classical feed-forward selects a branch by a classical outcome. Coherent control acts on a live quantum selector and must preserve coherence between its sectors. Each needs its own realization evidence.
  • Destructive readout accounts for the consumed owner. A retaining instrument requires the declared output owners and their conditional quantum state.
  • An implementation that reads out and then prepares qualifies only if the full correspondence equation holds.
  • A profile that supports only terminal readout may refuse a target claim that requires mid-circuit measurement and conditional quantum action.
  • Target lowering fails for a profile that lacks a required target feature or the evidence for it. The source retains its instrument, type and effect.
  • An engineering approximation requires the explicit separate claim.

For example, a program can measure both data qubits in Z and report their parity. On an even Bell state, that has the same classical distribution as std::measurement::parity_zz. The two readouts lose the coherence between 00 and 11, whereas the retaining parity projector preserves it. Preparing replacements in the computational basis leaves that difference in the even outcome map.

This abstract obligation is an input to the future target capability schema. QCP or another backend may express the premises in its own profile vocabulary. Source semantics are defined independently of that vocabulary.

Closed specialization

A generic definition describes a family. A realization is a claim about one member of that family. A closed specialization identifies the member. It fixes every choice that determines the meaning and interface of the member:

  • the resolved definition, with the complete identities of its dependencies and of its sources;
  • the ordered static arguments, including natural sizes and basis types;
  • the selected providers, their types, their capability requirements and their Meaning bindings;
  • all relevant static constraints and finite evaluation results;
  • the declared constitutional edition and the applicable semantic profile.

For a target claim, the realization fixes four more things: the target and profile, the ordered layout and encoding, the workspace contract and any choices that depend on the target.

Rules:

  • An unresolved item must not be treated as a successful concrete certificate. This holds for an unresolved parameter, capability requirement, constraint or budget expression.
  • A spelling, a bit width or a function name is not a specialization identity. The exact type tree is part of that identity, including Unit factors and zero-width owners.
  • Generic checking must cover the admitted cases of the generic body under its declared premises. A favorable specialization must not excuse an invalid generic body or erase an obligation in an unselected branch.
  • A well-typed family need not fit one fixed target at every parameter value.
  • A family theorem may quantify over all admissible substitutions. Its statement must retain those premises. It must not describe one fixed finite circuit as implementing an unbounded family.
  • Three properties are separate: static totality, the computation of the resource bounds of an instance, and the feasibility of those bounds on a target.

The static language specifies static evaluation and the forming of closed instances. Diagnostics states when a failed export names the closed specialization.

Logical axes and physical placement

A program orders the axes of its owners through its types. A target holds those axes at physical locations. This section states how a realization relates the two orders.

The logical interface records the ordered axes of each owner by the exact source type trees. A physical layout assigns the live axes to target locations. For example, the ordered pair of logical axes can occupy the physical locations (17,4). The first axis stays the first logical axis.

Take a pure instance with semantic action U and actual target action C. A correspondence with encodings E_in and E_out has the form C ∘ E_in = E_out ∘ U.

Rules:

  • Nested products and Bits<n> retain their declared axis order and owner order. A zero-width owner retains its interface identity and contributes no physical location.
  • A target realization records the initial and the final placement, or their equivalent ordered encodings.
  • The encodings include the specified workspace and its boundary state where required. The equation extends to arbitrary joint inputs with an untouched reference.
  • An observing realization uses the outcome maps. It records the placement and the disposition of the owners that are live at each interface, including any retired locations where the profile supports them.
  • Routing may change the placement from (17,4) to (4,17) by an emitted physical SWAP, provided the evidence tracks each logical axis into its location after the SWAP. The SWAP contributes to the target cost.
  • A metadata relabeling changes the description of the layout and leaves the physical state in place. The encoding evidence must justify that relabeling.
  • A source-level axis permutation changes the logical action. It must be included in U with its specified order and phase.
  • An emitted implementation of a logical SWAP must realize that action under its declared encodings.
  • The placement evidence identifies each routing step. It accounts for the live locations that routing and synthesis workspace use.

The target model may expose this relation to QCP routing analysis. The future target capability schema consumes this abstract correspondence. The bounded routing check covers literal physical SWAP intervals and their stated placement premises.

Workspace and exact correspondence

Workspace appears at two levels. The author writes source clean and dirty workspace, and it is part of the program’s contract and of its owner accounting. Backend workspace is additional storage that an implementation uses to realize that meaning.

For a unitary instance with clean workspace, a representative equation is

(C ⊗ I_E)(encode_in ⊗ I_E)(|0>_W ⊗ ψ)
  = (encode_out ⊗ I_E)(|0>_W ⊗ (U ⊗ I_E)ψ)

Here ψ belongs to L_in ⊗ E, and U maps L_in to L_out. The isometric encodings map W ⊗ L_in and W ⊗ L_out to the respective physical interfaces. The reference E stays last and is untouched. Each side therefore uses the same explicit order of workspace, data and reference.

Rules:

  • Backend storage must not become an implicit source owner or a permission to discard a source value. The realization claim must state how that storage is initialized, how long it lives, how it is restored and what it costs physically.
  • The equality is exact, including scalar phase. W starts and finishes in the specified pure zero state.
  • A type width, an owner name or an example input does not prove this equation.
  • For other encodings, or for differing input and output interfaces, the realization must state its corresponding typed equation explicitly.
  • Dirty workspace requires the declared identity action on its unknown state and on all reference correlations. Returning its marginal state on a few inputs is insufficient.
  • An observing implementation requires the complete instrument correspondence. That correspondence is unnormalized and indexed by outcome, and it includes the declared assembly of the classical result. A normalized selected branch or matching measurement probabilities must not replace it.
  • Projective equality differs from exact operator equality. Dropping a global phase can change a coherently controlled operation. The exact contract does not quotient by that phase.
  • A different equality relation must identify its boundary and its compositional obligations explicitly. The exact contract of this chapter has no approximate acceptance and no error budget.

The exact clean and dirty requirements come from the adopted QS interpretation. Exact target-domain membership and Separate engineering approximation claims state the rules of the exact and the approximate profiles.

Controlled target realizations

A controlled operation raises three separate questions: what it means, whether source code has access to it, and whether a target implements it. This section covers the third.

The coherent Meaning of controlled(U) is the ordered direct sum I ⊕ U, with inactive sector zero and active sector one. Semantic unitarity establishes the operator property of U. The static Controllable(U) judgment separately governs source use under the operation type rules.

Rules:

  • Target lowering requires one thing more: a checked realization of the exact direct sum on the selected target.
  • For a transparent implementation, a structural derivation must check each original gate or named implementation with its control, dependencies, axis order and exact phase.
  • The implementation must act as the identity in the inactive sector and must implement the requested operation in the active sector.
  • Composition and powers may compose those exact sector equations when the interfaces and the control match. Provider calls and the controlled powers that QPE uses carry the same obligations.
  • A realization certificate binds four things: the original operation and Meaning, the actual emitted implementation, the target profile, and the ordered encodings of the control, the data and the workspace. It identifies where the implementation uses native controlled gates and where it needs additional synthesis steps.
  • Physical counts include workspace preparation, live width, restoration, generated inverse steps and routing when the implementation requires them.
  • An opaque provider requires explicit controlled evidence. That evidence is bound to the requested operator and the emitted artifact.
  • A scalar phase that multiplies only the active block changes its phase relative to the inactive block. Projective agreement or channel equality for the uncontrolled operation therefore must not supply the controlled equation.
  • An engineering approximation to a controlled operator must bound its error against the intended controlled Meaning itself. It retains the exactness distinction of Separate engineering approximation claims.

Isometries and initialized target interfaces

A semantic isometry V: A -> B satisfies V† V = I_A. The basis trees of A and B can differ, including in width. This section covers the target realization of an isometry, including the wires that the target initializes.

Suppose a target implements the isometry with a unitary dilation. Let E_in: A -> P_in and E_out: B -> P_out be the exact isometric encodings of the input and of the output. Let C: P_in -> P_out be the action of the actual target circuit. On every joint input ψ with external reference R, the realization must establish

(C ⊗ I_R)(E_in ⊗ I_R) ψ = (E_out ⊗ I_R)(V ⊗ I_R) ψ.

The mathematical adjoint reverses V on its image. On the full output space, V V† is the projector onto that image. For an exact unitary dilation, C† E_out V = E_in, including under an arbitrary external reference. An available checked reverse circuit therefore recovers the encoded input on that image.

Rules:

  • A realization of Op<A -> B>, or of its refinement Op<A -> B, M>, retains both exact ports and the requested Meaning.
  • Sequential composition matches the middle tree exactly. Tensor composition preserves the declared order of both ports.
  • The input encoding specifies any initialized backend wires and their ordered placement.
  • The output encoding identifies the retained logical output. It fixes the state of every backend wire that the claim returns clean. Other physical degrees of freedom require an explicit output contract.
  • Isometry typing introduces no implicit discard and no partial trace.
  • Source clean and dirty contracts bind source owners separately.
  • A target claim accounts for every physical wire that the dilation uses, including initialized backend workspace. It also accounts for the preparation, realization, cleanup and routing that the selected target model requires.
  • A theorem that a dilation exists supplies a candidate. The accepted artifact requires the displayed correspondence.
  • An application outside the established image needs its own contract.
  • The static Adjointable capability and the physical realization of a reverse operation require independent evidence under the operation type rules.

Exact target-domain membership

A target profile does not realize every exact Meaning. Membership in the exact domain of a profile is the premise that an exact realization on that profile needs. M ∈ ExactDomain(P) is an abstract judgment about the requested exact Meaning M and a specified target profile P.

Rules:

  • The evidence identifies the profile and its version, the literal gate vocabulary, the coefficient and parameter domain, the ordered interface and the phase convention. It also states which assumptions the profile admits about initialization, encoding, workspace and restoration. A same-wire profile and a clean-workspace profile can therefore have different exact domains.
  • Physical Realizability requires evidence of this premise for an accepted exact target realization.
  • A checked exact witness for the profile may establish membership and bind the actual emitted artifact to the same Meaning.
  • An existence theorem or a coefficient test may support this judgment within its own hypotheses. A necessary condition alone does not authorize a candidate.
  • A search failure or a capacity refusal leaves the claim unproved.
  • A target-domain refusal concerns the requested realization. The source retains its exact meaning and typing.
  • A failed exact request does not select the engineering path implicitly. An engineering approximation requires an explicit separate claim with the applicable metric, a closed budget and the correspondence to the actual artifact.
  • This judgment is independent of any eventual QCP implementation. The future target capability schema may use it.

For example, take a profile whose gates and computational-basis coordinates have coefficients in Q(ζ8). Every entry of the gate product lies in that field. Appending clean zero workspace and taking the corresponding returned coordinate selects entries of the same product. A controlled phase of one sixteenth of a turn has coefficient ζ16 at its active diagonal entry, which is outside Q(ζ8). The obstruction applies for any number of such clean axes.

A target with a declared native sixteenth-turn gate requires its own exact-domain evidence. The eighth-root execution profile reports an unsupported exact phase for such a request.

Separate engineering approximation claims

An artifact that only approximates the meaning of a program is not an exact realization. A tool can state how close the artifact comes, as an engineering claim. This section keeps that claim apart from every exact one.

The adopted EXACT-2026-01 interpretation governs this boundary. An approximation with an epsilon bound is an engineering claim about synthesis or export. It lies outside the constitutional semantic truth conditions of edition 2026.

Rules:

  • An engineering claim must not replace the exact source Meaning, semantic equality, or the obligations of phase and control, of owner and effect, and of clean and dirty restoration.
  • An approximation bound grants no purity, no inverse access, no controlled access and no exact release.
  • A checked engineering claim must identify all of the following: the exact ideal, the actual artifact, the interface, the target and profile, the metric, the finite bound, the input and reference domain, and the composition assumptions.
  • An instrument claim must retain the complete public outcomes and the residual outputs with arbitrary external references.
  • Four kinds of information are distinct: information about sampling, about algorithmic resolution, about synthesis and about the numerical runtime. An absent claim does not mean zero error.
  • Engineering receipts, and their API and CLI claim kinds, must stay mechanically distinct from exact correspondence evidence and from resource evidence.
  • Neither a small bound nor a reported epsilon = 0 bypasses the independent exact gates or automatically discharges constitutional PR or target RS.
  • An actual circuit may independently obtain ordinary acceptance for its own exact meaning. That acceptance does not identify its meaning with the source ideal.
  • Reusing engineering evidence through a Meaning or an operation interface must not weaken the exact promise of that interface.
  • Numerical, interval and approximate search methods may construct an exact artifact or proof. That result is eligible for the separate exact correspondence and resource checks. The spelling of a search parameter does not decide its claim kind.
  • Accounting exactly for the cost of an approximate artifact does not supply its missing exact correspondence to the source.
  • Neither engineering validation nor this section creates an interpretation, a guarantee admission or a broader discharge of QS, PR or RS.

The bounded engineering profiles below implement these contracts within their stated domains. They define no universal approximation metric. Each bounded literal OpenQASM resource profile uses its own closed engineering budget.

Common rules of the native comparisons

Five qleisli engineering commands check an engineering claim through the native checker, the Lean checker executable. This section gives the rules that the five commands share. Each command takes one positional file and these options:

OptionMeaning
--edition=2026Required. It selects the constitutional edition.
--against=REQUESTRequired. It names the UTF-8 JSON request.
--lean-kernel=PATHOptional. It selects the native checker. Without it, the command uses the checker that QLEISLI_KERNEL names.
--format=jsonOptional. The command writes the result envelope in place of text.

Rules:

  • Every engineering comparison requires --edition=2026. A command without it fails with the code edition.
  • A command with neither --lean-kernel nor QLEISLI_KERNEL fails with the code kernel. A missing, incompatible or failed checker fails the comparison. No comparison falls back to another checker.
  • A command line with a repeated option, an unlisted option or an extra positional argument fails with the code usage and exit status 2. Every other failure has exit status 1.
  • Each request and each QIRF artifact must hold at least 1 byte and at most 16 MiB (limit). The QLV3 envelope retains its 64 MiB limit.
  • A request must hold exactly the fields of its format, with the fixed values of its profile. An unknown or missing field, an unknown version or another profile value is refused (format).
  • Each comparison accepts execution scope only. It refuses inspection scope and caller scope.
  • A report names the selected checker path. The checker identity is unpinned under the provenance limitation. A hash identifies the reported bytes. It establishes neither the correctness of the native binary nor human authority.
  • Serialized CLI output is an inert report. It is never reusable native acceptance.

Native pure engineering comparison

qleisli engineering compare-pure ACTUAL --edition=2026 --against=REQUEST [--lean-kernel=PATH] [--format=json]

This command checks that one pure artifact lies within a stated distance of an ideal pure artifact.

ACTUAL is an original finite QIRF artifact. REQUEST holds the rest of the claim. It holds the original ideal artifact, as a separate text. It holds the matrix and the ordered ports of each artifact, specified independently. It holds the complete type trees of the common domain and codomain. And it holds a budget, which is a closed canonical nonnegative dyadic number.

The request has exactly these fields:

FieldValue
format, versionqleisli.engineering-pure-request, 1
claim_kindengineering
metricpure-euclidean-operator-norm-v1
checker_profilefrobenius-r8-pure-leaf-v1
target_profileqirf-r8-pure-v1, an IR profile rather than a hardware claim
compositionsingle-component-v1
error_categoryimplementation-distance
domain, codomainFull QIRF Basis objects, for example {"tag":"bit"}
actual, idealObjects with exactly input, output and matrix
epsilon{"numerator":"7","denominator_bits":3} means 7/8; use reduced signed decimal data, with zero encoded as "0" and exponent 0
expected_artifactOriginal UTF-8 ideal QIRF text, including its original byte spelling

Rules:

  • Ports and matrices use the native pure-leaf request encodings.
  • The exact finite-matrix carrier is R8. The profile adds no arbitrary-angle syntax and no coefficient extension.
  • The comparison refuses a malformed or open budget, a false body matrix, and insufficient arithmetic or work capacity.
  • Hidden observation, reset or discard must not acquire a pure label through this path, even when the requested budget is large.

The checker establishes:

  • Each original artifact passes its ordinary check and then a fresh exact PureLeaf check against its own independent matrix request. The checker checks both complete type trees and the ordered ports. It compares the matrices only after these checks.
  • A squared Frobenius witness, over canonical rectangular R8 matrices, meets the closed nonnegative dyadic budget. The witness is sufficient for the explicitly selected Euclidean operator norm.

The checker does not establish:

  • A successful conservative witness is an inequality, not a completeness claim. When a witness fails, the failure does not establish that no approximation or synthesis exists.
  • Neither check changes the exact equality gate.
  • The result does not establish source preservation, exact source correspondence, an instrument bound or a target-resource bound. The report states each of them as unestablished.
  • The report cannot be converted to AcceptedProgram, a Meaning, inverse or controlled access, cleanup evidence or resource evidence. This holds at epsilon 0 too.

The text report and the JSON report identify the engineering metric, the bound, the profiles and the composition. They also identify the digests of the original artifacts and of the request, and the selected checker path. They report three kinds of error separately as not-claimed: sampling, algorithmic resolution and numerical runtime. Those errors are not silently zero.

Implementation note. The Rust interchange::engineering_pure API constructs data-only requests. It returns a private, allocation-bound EngineeringPureReport after a fresh native check. Its serialized convenience entry independently decodes the same typed request. The native checker compares the typed context and both fresh graphs with the actual retained Rust execution description. A separate native engineering protocol binds both checks to the actual invocation and to both retained host graphs. The engineering response uses a distinct header and cannot enter an exact accepted-handle constructor.

Native ordered pure tensor comparison

qleisli engineering compare-pure-tensor ACTUAL --edition=2026 --against=REQUEST [--lean-kernel=PATH] [--format=json]

This command checks a bound for an artifact that is the ordered tensor of two components, where each component has its own budget.

This comparison combines exactly two ordered pure comparisons. It checks six original QIRF artifacts afresh: the actual and ideal parents, the actual and ideal left components, and the actual and ideal right components.

The request has exactly fourteen top-level fields:

FieldValue
format, versionqleisli.engineering-pure-tensor-request, 1
claim_kindengineering-pure-tensor-bound
metricpure-euclidean-operator-norm-v1
checker_profiletensor-additive-r8-pure-leaf-v1
target_profileqirf-r8-pure-v1
compositionordered-tensor-two-v1
error_categoryimplementation-distance
actual, idealParent leaf objects with exactly input, output, matrix
left, rightComponent objects described below, in that order
epsilonCanonical nonnegative dyadic, exactly the sum of the two component budgets
expected_artifactOriginal UTF-8 ideal parent QIRF text

Each component object has exactly domain, codomain, actual, ideal, epsilon, actual_artifact and expected_artifact.

Rules:

  • Types, leaf requests and budgets use the encodings of the pure comparison. Both artifact fields of a component hold the original UTF-8 QIRF text.
  • Each body retains its own dependency order, exact matrix request, ordered ports and full type trees.
  • Both parent interfaces must be the complete products of the component interfaces. Unit nodes are retained even when they occupy no physical wires. The checker derives the parent type trees from the ordered components. A width-only claim does not supply them.
  • The exact parent equations use the low-first coordinates left + left_dimension * right. Both parent matrices must equal the ordered tensors of their respective component matrices.
  • The comparison refuses missing or extra components, altered profiles, noncanonical or open budgets, a stale total budget, false body matrices, and arithmetic or work exhaustion. It refuses a false parent tensor equation (contract).
  • The limits of the pure comparison apply: the limit on each file, on the envelope, on a matrix and on work.

The checker establishes:

  • The checker adds the two closed component bounds exactly. It requires the sum to equal the explicitly requested total bound.
  • It does not repeat the sufficient Frobenius inequality on the whole parent. An additive component certificate may succeed when that whole-matrix witness is too loose.
  • All six body checks, the component inequalities, the parent equations and the bounded addition share one work counter.
  • The checked additive operator bound extends to every finite external reference, including entangled inputs. It does so under the freshly checked exact isometry premises.

The checker does not establish:

  • The result supplies no exact equality between the parents, no source preservation, no inverse or controlled access, no cleanup, no correspondence to an emitted target and no resource certificate. This holds at total epsilon zero too.
  • This comparison supplies only its stated additive bound for two components in one ordered tensor. It checks no recursive, sequential, controlled or instrument composition. Support of the native comparisons lists the composition that each comparison checks.

The text report and the JSON report state this engineering bound and its composition profile. They carry the same separate categories of unclaimed error as the pure comparison, and the same disclosures about preservation and resources.

Implementation note. In the dedicated QLV3 invocation, the native checker compares its freshly decoded typed request and all six graphs with the retained Rust QDV1 view. The order is parent-actual, parent-ideal, left-actual, left-ideal, right-actual, right-ideal. The private Rust report binds the immutable artifacts, the original request and the selected checker path. A changed component, matrix, budget or parent cannot reuse it.

Instrument engineering comparison

qleisli engineering compare-instrument ACTUAL --edition=2026 --against=REQUEST [--lean-kernel=PATH] [--format=json]

This command checks that an actual instrument lies within a stated distance of an ideal instrument.

The instrument comparison uses a complete adjoint Euclidean operator bound. Take the Kraus families of the actual and of the ideal instrument. The bound limits the difference between their adjoint actions on every joint observable family B, for every finite external reference R and classical input x:

norm(sum_y,h (K_actual[x,y,h] tensor I_R)^dagger B[y]
             (K_actual[x,y,h] tensor I_R)
   - sum_y,k (K_ideal[x,y,k] tensor I_R)^dagger B[y]
             (K_ideal[x,y,k] tensor I_R)) <= epsilon * norm(B)_infinity

The norms on operators are explicitly Euclidean operator norms. The family norm is the supremum over public outcomes. The two families may have different sets of hidden histories. The bound quantifies over joint observables, including correlations with the reference. It has no factor for the dimension of the reference, and it does not renormalize branches. The metric is not advertised as a diamond norm without a separate proof of norm duality.

The request has exactly fourteen fields:

FieldValue
format, versionqleisli.engineering-instrument-request, 1
claim_kindengineering-instrument-bound
metriccomplete-adjoint-euclidean-operator-norm-v1
checker_profilechoi-l1-r8-instrument-v1
target_profileqirf-r8-instrument-v1, an IR profile, not hardware
compositionsingle-component-v1
error_categoryimplementation-distance
actual_signature, expected_signatureThe complete requested signatures
identityThe original identity data
epsilonClosed dyadic data, for example {"numerator":"1","denominator_bits":2} for 1/4
majorantsThe witness described below
expected_artifactThe original ideal QIRF text

Rules:

  • The witness assigns a canonical nonnegative closed dyadic q to every complex CP-coefficient difference. The order is fixed: public outcome, output row, input column, other output row, other input column.
  • All actual and ideal public labels contribute. An absent label denotes zero. The witness length must be exact.
  • The comparison refuses malformed dimensions or coefficients, noncanonical or negative budgets, insufficient witnesses, and arithmetic or work exhaustion. It refuses an epsilon that the majorants exceed (contract).
  • Nothing that a producer supplies replaces reconstruction. This holds for outcome labels, matrices, probabilities and success flags.
  • The comparison refuses old outer frames and cross-kind replies.

The checker establishes:

  • On both bodies, the checker reuses the ordinary steps: the root and dependency checks, the equality with the requested complete signature, the ordered result projection, the principal Observe checks, and the complete Kraus and Gram reconstruction.
  • The checker reconstructs each coefficient afresh from all matching hidden histories. Exact R8 arithmetic checks its squared magnitude against q*q. Exact summation checks sum(q) <= epsilon. Both q and epsilon have unsquared norm units.
  • The profile has one quantum input owner, empty classical inputs and the bounded dense reconstruction. The independent metric also defines general finite classical input families, for later composition work.

The checker does not establish:

  • The result leaves these unestablished: pure phase agreement, source preservation, inverse or controlled access, cleanup, resources and constitutional PR or RS. This holds at epsilon zero too. No instrument bound grants exact release.
  • Requested source and type trees are distinct from source-to-IR preservation.
  • The profile is single-component. It issues no composed certificate, and it checks no resource limit. A comparison of emitted target bytes requires the literal OpenQASM profile.
  • Sampling, algorithmic resolution and numerical runtime error are separate unclaimed categories. The pure comparison reports instrument bounds as unestablished.

Text and --format=json disclose the complete instrument bound, the digests of the actual inputs and the unpinned checker identity. For example:

qleisli engineering compare-instrument actual.qirf --edition=2026 --against=comparison.json --lean-kernel=PATH

Implementation note. The Rust request uses validated InstrumentSignature and InstrumentResult data. Its private EngineeringInstrumentReport retains both immutable artifacts, the original request and the selected checker path. The native QLV3 invocation compares both fresh graphs, the signatures, the identity and the complete budget and witness data with the Rust execution view. Quantitative resource claims

A realization says that an artifact implements a program. A resource claim says how much the artifact uses, for example its live width or its gate count. This section gives the rules for such claims.

Resource Safety is the third permanent obligation of edition 2026. The adopted resource interpretation applies to finite quantitative bounds on the actual accepted program and its claimed realization. A resource certificate states a checked bound for one emitted artifact. The subsections below describe the bounded checks of the canonical terminal OpenQASM profile.

Rules:

  • A resource certificate must identify all of the following: the actual closed instance, the emitted artifact, the target and profile, the resource measures, the input and scheduling premises, and the checked bounds over all admitted execution paths.
  • A change to the providers, the layout, the target, the workspace or the output bytes invalidates reuse, unless a checked correspondence establishes the changed claim.
  • A content hash establishes identity, not the bound.
  • The provider resource contract applies to each selected body and composition. An external, opaque or device-native implementation must meet the same admission requirements.
  • Gate count, depth and non-Clifford cost require declared target conventions. Literal IR T-word counts are not automatically physical or optimal T counts.
  • Five things are different claims: compiler memory, checker work limits, static termination, ownership safety and target resource bounds. In particular, the ownership-only predicate does not discharge quantitative RS.
  • A closed finite specialization can meet its stated obligations by checked accounting on its actual artifact. A family can use a symbolic bound such as Resource(P<n>) <= f(n) within edition 2026. Its proof must cover the claimed parameters and the actual compilation and resource binding.
  • Resource behavior outside current coverage can occur in a sized family, bounded runtime control, specialization, target synthesis or provider composition. The Guardian interprets the applicable obligation under the existing edition. The required proofs of the bound and of preservation must pass before the scope can claim constitutional conformance. Previously admitted guarantees continue to bind the current acceptance path.
  • A failure to meet a target budget rejects that target claim. It does not change the mathematical denotation of the program.
  • A quantitative claim about a behavior-preserving realization requires a separate resource certificate.

The QCP implementation and concrete target schemas have a separately scheduled scope. This chapter is not their implementation.

Physical live width

This measure counts the physical locations that an implementation keeps live.

Rules:

  • Physical live width includes logical data, explicit source workspace, backend synthesis workspace, and routing and target storage.
  • A count takes each actual physical location once. It accounts for the lifetime of the location and its permitted reuse.
  • Peak width is the maximum simultaneous live count. A sum of separate peaks is a conservative upper bound, not necessarily the realized peak.
  • Logical width alone does not bound the physical implementation width.

Exclusive access and generated work

This section says how a bound counts the wires of an exclusive access and the work that an implementation generates.

An indexed exclusive call partitions its parent for a time into the selected owners and the remainder owners. The caller resumes with the reassembled parent. The exit from this access keeps its wires live. Logical owner identity and exact clean return belong to Soundness. Count every active physical location for its whole live interval.

Include any generated preparation, inverse calls, uncomputation and physical routing in the bound for the implementation that uses them. A replacement bound must be checked on the actual replacement artifact against the applicable target budget and capacity. Structural splitting and joining rearrange the logical owner map. Physical SWAPs contribute to the target count.

Implementation note. The bounded indexed Raw proof RuntimeExclusiveResources.indexed_access_live_wires relates the actual access boundaries to the original owner map and counts each live wire once. routed_prefix_live_wires checks that accounting throughout each structural split/join route. The inner operation has its own resource obligations. The terminal placement check replays the emitted gate word and checks its actual width and costs, including caller spectators and checked scratch.

Routing segments

A routing check covers an interval of literal physical SWAPs. A fixed-occupancy SWAP segment records the ordered placement of all occupied locations. The record includes any scratch that is live during the segment, whether it belongs to the source, to synthesis or to routing.

Rules:

  • Each literal SWAP must name two distinct occupied locations that are connected in the declared profile.
  • A SWAP moves both locations in the placement and leaves every other location alone. It counts as one operation even when a later SWAP cancels it.
  • The final placement preserves the logical axis order and the exact joint coefficients with any reference.
  • The check of a segment does not establish that the interval is used for routing, or that its entry placement is correct. A complete certificate establishes both. It also checks the other target operations, allocation, release and reuse.

Implementation note. The native checker independently decodes the declared register width and the literal SWAP instructions from a designated byte interval of canonical OpenQASM output. It also checks the target capacity.

The canonical terminal profile

The canonical terminal profile reserves all declared physical locations and resets them before its first gate. It keeps the reservation through terminal measurement.

Rules:

  • The independent reader of the complete artifact counts from the output bytes. It counts reserved and reset wires, gates, literal SWAPs, T/TDG and measurements.
  • An exact finite workspace check may compare a designated gate interval with the independently checked original clean or dirty body. The comparison uses the declared ordered placement of data and scratch. The physical register must cover the checked Raw peak.
  • The checker can derive the ordered locations of the workspace from the checked original Raw frame and its complete injective physical placement.
  • A fresh scratch must occupy a location absent from that frame. For a fresh scratch wire, the checker can verify that nothing touches it between the initial reset and workspace entry, or after exact restoration.

Placement replay

Take a closed straight-line Raw prefix with zero preparations, structural owner operations and finite gates. For such a prefix, the checker derives the original frame by replay. It replays the original prefix and the decoded target gate word.

Rules:

  • Marked routing operations must match connected physical SWAPs in the emitted word. The checker matches an ordinary Raw SWAP as a gate, and that SWAP does not move the encoding.
  • The derived original frame must equal the checked workspace entry, including zero-width ports.
  • With one checked workspace interval and literal gates, the checker can carry the original exit owners through the target suffix. It uses the resulting placement to verify the emitted terminal measurements in classical output order.
  • All original quantum owners must be consumed by the end. Discarded owners and measurements outside the classical outputs do not add to the emitted readout.
  • The single-interval check requires an exclusive lifetime for fresh scratch.
  • Outside a workspace interval, the checker can compare a single original circuit event against a decomposed target word. It derives the ordered support from the original operands and controls. It compares the complete exact operators on that support, up to six axes.
  • The checker rejects gates on other wires and phase changes. Matrix checking spends the same cumulative work as the surrounding replay.
  • The replay can align a target word with several consecutive Raw circuit events and structural owner operations. It reads the actual owner transitions and checks the combined operator on the ordered support. Preparation, observation and source workspace boundaries require their own alignment.
  • A structural basis lift updates the physical placement only when its complete table is an axis permutation. It contributes zero gates. A routing SWAP must occupy a literal gate position.
  • For a Q<Bits<n>> data register, each original take_bit or put_bit index determines the ordered wire selection or insertion. The Raw repartition retains the other owners, including a distinct Q<Bits<0>> remainder. Repartition inside a workspace body is part of its independently evaluated exact operator.

Synthesis scratch

A gate decomposition may use allocated synthesis scratch. These rules say where that scratch sits and how the checker confirms that it returns to zero.

Rules:

  • The physical locations of the scratch must be declared and separate from all live owners.
  • The checker compares the full target word on the data and the scratch with the original operator, under the canonical zero embedding at both ends. The executable check keeps the same zero encoding at entry and exit.
  • The six-axis bound includes scratch.
  • A slice of several aligned events that uses synthesis scratch has the same zero-return check.
  • A complete trace may reserve locations for several disjoint synthesis slices. Original allocation, routing, source workspace and readout must not touch these locations. Every slice that uses them checks its own zero return.
  • An individual slice may instead use exclusive scratch. The checker verifies that the scratch is untouched everywhere else.

Reuse of clean scratch

A physical scratch location may be reused across ordered, disjoint, clean intervals.

Rules:

  • The checker requires an exact zero-return equation for each interval. An interval with zero gates also checks that equation, and no gate touches the location in it.
  • Every gate that touches the location must lie in one of those intervals. The location must be absent from terminal readout.
  • The literal multi-interval check derives every entry placement from the preceding original owners and target word. It reserves each designated scratch location across the complete trace. It keeps the location out of ordinary preparations, routing and terminal readout.
  • Each reuse interval must be bound to its own checked original workspace.
  • The written logical operation and the workspace body use the same admitted workspace source fragment. An independently checked controlled phase may therefore appear in the declared logical operation and in the body. Their exact restoration equation checks the complete action on the data and the scratch.

A successful native placement check has these properties:

  • Each reusable clean wire is in the counted reset register.
  • Every gate that touches that wire belongs to a checked restoration interval, with one operator step per decoded gate in the interval.
  • A routing action before a workspace or in the terminal suffix points to a literal SWAP on an allowed edge at a counted gate index.
  • The reported SWAP total includes the literal routing gates and any ordinary SWAP instructions in the target word.

Implementation note. The typed-source adapter reads workspace intervals from the original checked root and named calls. It binds each local interval to the identical Raw operations at its position in the root. It checks the selected and returned ports against the full caller frame and retains all untouched spectator ports. The exporter preserves the interval boundaries across exact rewrites. It proposes the corresponding gate positions and scratch locations. The native checker checks the proposal against the original source, including the clean and dirty intervals and the terminal result order.

Computed scopes

A with_computed interval holds forward steps, protected uses and inverse steps. A resource count covers all three.

Take a structural with_computed interval that the source check has accepted. The Raw resource read counts the full live frame plus one private scratch wire at peak. It counts one preparation, one clean release and both predicate passes. The literal circuit count includes every forward step, inverse step and protected use.

The terminal OpenQASM placement check can read this event in a closed original source root. This includes a pure interval before or after an explicit workspace.

Rules:

  • The check places the ordered source wires, followed by a private predicate wire. It reconstructs the whole forward, protected-use and inverse word from the Raw operation.
  • The decoded target word must agree on the complete support, including the predicate wire, with exact phase.
  • If the target decomposition uses additional scratch, the same synthesis check requires the scratch to start at zero and return to zero.
  • The predicate wire and the synthesis wires are reserved. Source allocation, routing and terminal readout do not use them.

Phase words and target-dependent costs

One phase can cost a different number of gates on different targets. This section fixes the phase words of the eighth-root profile and states what their counts show.

In the eighth-root finite profile, let ω = exp(2πi/8). The one-axis phase with residue r is exactly diag(1,ω^r) on the declared axis. This includes an action on an input that is entangled with a reference.

Rules:

  • A supported source phase[j,d] has r = 8j / 2^d when this quotient is an integer. The primitive contract specifies the source and profile limits.
  • Adjoint changes the exponent to -r modulo eight. Repetition adds the residue on each action.
  • A scalar ω^r I differs from a one-axis phase. It can become a relative phase under coherent control.
  • The finite format represents the one-axis phase by a monomial with the chosen axis, the identity permutation [0,1] and the phase exponents [0,r]. The action of the historical word of r T gates is the same diagonal.
  • A decoder must keep decoding the gate and circuit constructors in their versioned profiles. The equivalent monomial representation requires no dedicated Phase constructor and no change to the established encoding of a T word.

Small obstruction examples

A program can be semantically unitary and yet have no implementation on the same wires in a stated gate vocabulary. This section shows two small cases and the determinant argument behind them.

Embedding a k-qubit gate G into n wires, under a fixed ordered basis, gives determinant det(G)^(2^(n-k)). Determinants multiply under composition. Consequently the generator set of a target restricts the determinants that it reaches on the same wires.

Rules:

  • This restriction is a necessary condition. Sufficiency requires a separate theorem for that precise generator, phase and workspace profile.
  • For a basis-permutation vocabulary, signs multiply under composition. An even-only vocabulary therefore excludes odd permutations on the same wires.
  • The precomputed determinant classes of the generators, or the parity of an available permutation table, may reject a candidate before synthesis.
  • The obstructions use the stated generator vocabulary, controls and workspace premises.

A diagnostic for a failed realization claim or resource claim reports a restriction on the realization. Diagnostics and recovery gives the codes and the fields.

Rules:

  • It must distinguish a proved obstruction from unsupported synthesis and from exhausted checking capacity.
  • An unsupported target operation or a target-width error must not be presented as a failure of semantic unitarity.
  • The terminal exporter reports an unsupported operation (unsupported) and the capacity limit of the synthesis workspace (limit). It reports its own target refusal after source checking has completed.

Code:

  • The native comparisons, the placement replay and the resource report are bounded implementation checks. Each covers the profile that its section states.
  • General specialization certificates and general diagnostic coverage are open work.

Tests:

  • The regression named in Small obstruction examples covers the two obstruction examples.
  • A Rust and Lean regression binds the same emitted OpenQASM bytes to two original clean intervals. It checks their placement, exact restoration and readout.
  • The independent scripts/test_review_v024.py oracle checks emitted small QFT and control circuits, including complex phase, output axes and zero-return scratch. Such bounded numerical validation is distinct from a general transformation proof and from a resource certificate.

Proof, for the QPTL library:

  • controlled_mul proves the operator identity that composes the exact sector equations of a controlled composition.
  • controlled_pow proves the corresponding operator identity for powers.
  • controlled_phase_ne proves that a scalar phase on the active block alone changes the controlled operator. It holds for any nontrivial scalar on a nonzero operator.
  • dilation_recover_reference derives the recovery equation of an isometry, restricted to its image. It derives the equation from the exact forward square and the unitary implementation law.
  • extracted_sixteenth_ne proves the coefficient obstruction of Exact target-domain membership.
  • QPTL.FiniteMatrix.det_embedded proves the determinant formula of an embedded gate, for finite matrix bases and simultaneous reindexing of both interfaces.
  • QPTL.FiniteMatrix.exact_same_wire_obstruction covers a product of generators whose determinants lie in a set that is closed under multiplication and contains one. Such a product cannot equal a requested matrix with determinant outside that set. The premises retain the chosen target vocabulary, layout and exact phase.

Proof, for source workspace on the finite one-scratch checker:

  • WorkspaceTargetAction.clean_checked_separates uses the exact checked insertion and output coefficients with the fixed-scratch law of QPTL. The joint output density has support only in the zero-scratch block, for every input density on data and a reference.
  • WorkspaceTargetAction.dirty_checked_promotion binds every coefficient of the actual body to the logical operator promoted on the ordered data axes.
  • WorkspaceTargetAction.dirty_checked_reference applies that action to each joint reference column. It proves that every selected input coordinate retains the scratch bit of the output.
  • Qleisli.WorkspaceTargetAction.decoded_action connects the exact action of a decoded workspace slice to the literal Raw body on its full ordered data and scratch interface. The equality preserves the original phase for arbitrary reference columns.
  • native_clean_access_counted_once covers each reserved clean wire. It links native placement success to the literal reset of the wire and to one unique restoration interval for every emitted gate that touches it. It also places the interval inside the counted target gates and excludes the scratch wire from terminal readout.

Proof, for synthesis scratch:

  • WorkspaceTargetAction.synthesis_action connects the actual decoded gate word to the original local operator under the canonical zero encoding. It proves equality with full phase on every external reference column. The amplitude is zero whenever any synthesis scratch bit is nonzero. The checked placement replay recovers this evidence at the original Raw position and physical gate offset, for a single circuit event or an optimized span.
  • WorkspaceTargetAction.bound_synthesis_action connects the replay before a source workspace to that exact action.
  • WorkspaceTargetAction.native_suffix_synthesis_action connects the terminal suffix of the native decision to that exact action.
  • In both theorems, the equation uses the original circuit steps on the recovered support with their dependencies. Reindexing the checked local steps by the ordered layout of data and synthesis scratch recovers the same emitted instruction slice. The decoder establishes the arity and the distinct, in-range operands of each instruction.
  • The full physical register action on a fixed spectator slice therefore agrees with the checked local word, for arbitrary input amplitudes and external references. The literal gate interval and its synthesis scratch locations are within the reported gate and register counts.

Proof, for the resource report:

  • A Lean theorem proves that the decoded register width bounds the maximum Raw live width over the workspace occurrences that the replay visits. The bound includes caller spectators and temporary clean wires.
  • The native reply theorem binds the reported counts to the exact target bytes. It proves that the reserved width is within the requested target capacity.
  • Qleisli.TerminalPlacement.native_terminal_correspondence relates the same successful native decision to the selected original Raw root. Take the moment immediately before terminal measurement, after all decoded gates have run from reset. At that moment the entire physical complex column agrees with the corresponding pure Raw prefix under its ordered placement. The agreement includes phase and restored scratch.
  • By the same theorem, the final measurement distribution agrees for every public result word after summing the original hidden outcomes. The theorem also binds the resource counts of the reply and its workspace peak bound to that same checked packet.

Proof, for computed scopes:

  • Qleisli.RuntimeComputedResources.structural_counts binds the counts of a structural with_computed interval to the same original source-checked Raw occurrence. It retains caller spectators in the peak width.
  • QleisliKernel.Qirf.StructuralComputedRaw.read_work proves the debit of the executable Raw interval reader.
  • QleisliKernel.Qirf.StructuralComputedUse.read_circuit_work identifies the work state after the source-bound compute and inverse comparisons and before the check of the emitted auxiliary action.
  • The same literal XOR factorization proves the logical action for the independently checked Raw event, even for an arbitrary caller amplitude function. The source-bound theorem additionally identifies the predicate and the phase from the original body. The complete pure Raw event fold uses the local logical action without constructing a global matrix.
  • BoundSegment.structural_paid and FinishedTrace.suffix_structural_paid each recover the checked placement and the gate interval from the executable replay.
  • StructuralPlacement.literal_costs binds that interval, and the distinct data and scratch locations, to the decoded gate and reset counts.
  • ProtectedTablePlacement.literal_costs ties the decoded interval of a finite-table computed scope, and its distinct physical locations, to the reported gate and reset counts.

Proof, for engineering claims:

  • The proof of the pure comparison bounds the explicitly selected Euclidean operator norm, including arbitrary finite external references and literal scalar phase.
  • Proofs of pure composition establish additive error rules for sequential composition and for ordered tensor composition. The rules use the same Euclidean metric and the stated exact isometry premises, and they include arbitrary references.
  • The coherent-control corollary additionally requires equal domain and codomain and the independent access meanings.
  • These mathematical rules do not change the single-component profile, and they supply no compiler or lowering preservation.
  • Independent mathematical composition of instruments uses the actual Kraus families. Full Gram completeness implies adjoint contraction at every finite reference.
  • A literal pure operator gap delta and both pure Gram equations imply the one-way instrument bound 2*delta. No phase information is recovered.
  • Feed-forward composition retains the public (y,z) outcomes and the hidden (h,k) histories. Ordered tensor composition retains the declared left and right input, output, public and hidden products through explicit proved reference regrouping.
  • Both constructions have additive epsilon_1 + epsilon_2 bounds under their stated component bounds and Gram premises.
  • Separate completeness theorems require both original Gram equations. A bound alone does not establish them.
  • No compiler consumes these results. They give no proof that composition is preserved for original source or QIRF.

Preservation:

  • An engineering result does not establish source preservation or target preservation. A location in a diagnostic supplies no certificate.
  • The two admitted QLV1 guarantees cover original-root ownership and classical scope. They do not certify source lowering, target emission or quantitative bounds.
  • QS-2026-01, PR-2026-01, RS-2026-01 and EXACT-2026-01 are pending, as the initial guarantees record. No statement of this chapter discharges one of them, and this chapter admits no guarantee.

Specification review:

  • The standard gate interpretation of the literal OpenQASM profiles is an explicit specification assumption. This chapter records no specification review of its rules.
