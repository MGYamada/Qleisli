# V1 algorithm goals

Six algorithm families guide the design of Qleisli 1.0.0. The names below
identify proposed standard-library APIs. The shared goal is to express the
algorithms in ordinary checked Qleisli where possible and to verify and count
operations from the program's structure as instances grow.

## Prime factorization

`std::arithmetic::prime_factorize<N>` targets integers greater than one that
fit in `N` bits. A successful result represents the prime factors with their
multiplicities. A Shor-style realization uses reversible modular exponentiation
and phase estimation to find the period of powers of a chosen coprime base.
Classical postprocessing derives and verifies candidate factors.

The design calls for typed results, ordinary control flow for retries and
failure, and resource accounting across attempts. Reversible arithmetic
subroutines must compose while returning clean workspace to zero.
[Design issue #318](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/318)

## Phase estimation

`std::spectral::phase_estimate<P>` targets a `P`-bit classical estimate of the
phase `θ modulo 1` of a unitary on a supplied eigenstate. Acting on that state,
the unitary multiplies it by `exp(2πiθ)`. For a supported phase of the form
`k / 2^P`, the ideal estimate encodes `k` with probability one. Other supported
phases have a specified output distribution.

The standard realization uses checked access to controlled powers of the
unitary and a reusable inverse quantum Fourier transform.
[Design issue #319](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/319)

## Marked search

`std::search::find_marked<N>` targets the search for an `N`-bit value that
satisfies a supplied predicate. A Grover-style realization starts from a uniform
superposition and uses phase reflections to increase the probability of
measuring a marked value. Classical verification checks the measured candidate
against the predicate before returning it as a witness.

The iteration schedule depends on what the realization knows about the number
of marked values. An unsuccessful attempt reports the lack of a verified
witness from that attempt. Establishing that the marked set is empty requires
its own evidence.
[Design issue #320](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/320)

## Markov-chain quantization

`std::markov::quantize` targets a discrete-time quantum walk from a classical
Markov chain. For each state `x`, the transition probability `P(x,y)` determines
the amplitude `√P(x,y)` of the successor state `y` in a coherent encoding of
the transition row. A Szegedy-style construction composes two reflections built
from the transition isometry to produce a walk unitary that can be used by
phase estimation or search.

The v1 target covers instances whose required amplitudes fit the exact
coefficient ring generated by roots of unity of power-of-two order and by
`1/2`. Broader coefficient domains and continuous-time walks are v1+ goals.
[Design issue #321](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/321)

## Quantum singular value transformation

`std::spectral::qsvt` targets a transformation of the singular values of an
operator supplied as a **block encoding**, where a designated block of a
unitary represents the operator after scaling. The circuit combines uses of
that unitary with phase operations. Its designated output block encodes the
transformation determined by the supplied phase sequence.

The v1 target takes exact phase data from the supported coefficient ring,
including dyadic phases such as `exp(iπ/8)`. The contract describes the
transformation induced by that sequence. Deriving a sequence from a desired
polynomial is a separate synthesis task.
[Design issue #322](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/322)

## Amplitude estimation

`std::spectral::amplitude_estimate` targets the probability of a designated
success outcome for a coherently prepared state. For a state `ψ` and a projector
`Π` onto the success subspace, the quantity to estimate is `‖Πψ‖²`. The result
has a defined decoding rule and a precision and confidence guarantee.

The chosen circuit has its own exact output distribution. The precision and
confidence describe the statistical error of the decoded estimate. The resource
contract counts calls to preparation and success-marking operations, along with
repetitions and measurements.
[Design issue #323](https://github.com/MGYamada/Qleisli/issues/323)
